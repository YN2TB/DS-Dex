## Findings so far

- **⚠️ Ch. 01's best addition, and Mankiw explicitly declines it:** the book says the trade price is "beyond the scope of this chapter". **Computing the surplus split across the feasible range [2,4] shows the TOTAL GAIN IS CONSTANT AT 10 oz of potatoes at every price** — at P=2 Ruby captures nothing, at P=4 Frank captures nothing, at Mankiw's P=3 they split evenly (**a choice of example, not a result**). ⇒ **the price is purely DISTRIBUTIVE: comparative advantage determines whether there are gains and who specialises, and says NOTHING about who captures them.** *Corollary worth repeating: "trade raises the total" is robust; "trade helps everyone" does not follow.*
- **⚠️ Ch. 01 proved (not asserted) that absolute advantage is irrelevant — by scaling.** Multiply BOTH of Ruby's productivities by k=2,5,10: her opportunity costs stay **exactly** 2 and ½ because an opp cost is a **ratio** and k cancels. **The trade pattern never changes.** Then varying only the RATIO: at 40 min/oz meat the two have identical opportunity costs and **the gains from trade are EXACTLY ZERO**. ⇒ **difference is the source of gain — not skill, not wealth, not productivity.**
- **⚠️ Ch. 01's cross-subject link, and it's strong: Commercial Banking ch. 09's interest-rate swap IS comparative advantage.** The high-rated borrower is cheaper in **both** markets (absolute advantage in everything, exactly like Ruby) and the entire **0.75%** gain comes from the spread difference (2.50% fixed vs 1.75% floating). **Equalise the spreads → gain is zero**, the exact analogue of the 40-min row. **Neither book mentions the other.**

## ⚠️ THE OPERATOR CIPHER — read this before extracting anything

**Mankiw's PDFs render every arithmetic operator as a digit.** Equations extract looking like lists of numbers and are **silently wrong**.

| extracts as | actually means |
|---|---|
| `5` | **=** |
| `1` | **+** |
| `2` | **−** |
| `3` | **×** |

**`Y 5 C 1 I 1 G 1 NX` is $Y = C + I + G + NX$.**

**⚠️ It is NOT a fixed substitution — the same digit means different things in one line.** The midpoint elasticity formula extracts as `Price elasticity of demand 5 (Q2 2 Q1) / [(Q2 1 Q1) / 2]`, i.e. $\frac{Q_2-Q_1}{(Q_2+Q_1)/2}$ — **six digits, four meanings** (subscript, minus, subscript, plus, subscript, literal 2). And `(6 2 4) / 5 3 100 5 40` is $(6-4)/5\times100=40$, where **`5` is a literal number AND an equals sign in the same expression.**

**There is no mechanical decoder. You must know the economics to read the maths.** **Rule: never transcribe a formula — reconstruct it from the surrounding prose, then verify it numerically against the book's own worked figures.**

**Verified during setup** — in the macro book: the GDP identity, the quantity equation (`M 3 V 5 P 3 Y`), the Fisher equation (`Real 5 Nominal 2 Inflation`), and the CPI basket (decoded to \$8/\$14/\$20, all three reproduce exactly); independently in the micro book: both elasticity formulas.

**Other faults:** possessive apostrophes mangle (`today's`→`todayrs`); short phrases duplicate (`which relates Y, which relates Y`); running heads interleave. **Parkin & Bade does NOT share the cipher** — it extracts cleanly, equations included.

**⚠️ Every figure is an image and lost — worst-case subject for this**, since Mankiw teaches almost entirely through shifting curves and shaded areas. Captions and stray axis labels survive and **look like data**, which is worse than nothing. **Numerical tables are images too: recompute, never transcribe.**
