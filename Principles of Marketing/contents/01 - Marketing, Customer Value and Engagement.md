# Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement

**Marketing is not advertising, and it is not selling.** **Kotler's definition is a *loop*: the process by which companies engage customers, build strong relationships, and create customer value ***in order to capture value from customers in return***.**

**Three results.**

**§3 — ⚠️ the selling and marketing concepts are not weak and strong versions of one idea.** **They run the causal arrow in opposite directions** — one starts with the existing product, the other with the customer. **Which is why "we do a lot of marketing" usually means "we do a lot of selling".**

**§4 — ⚠️ Kotler's \$50,000 customer lifetime value verified — and it is *revenue*, not value.** *(Computed: discounting at 8% gives **\$33,550**; applying a 25% gross margin gives **\$8,388**; adding an 80% annual retention rate gives **\$3,571**. **The headline overstates by a factor of fourteen.**)* **⚠️ And defect (1) is exactly the error [[Monetary and Financial Theories/contents/02 - The Meaning of Interest Rates|Monetary Theories ch. 02]] caught in a lottery advertisement.**

**§5 — ⚠️ "Sales and market share reflect the past; customer equity suggests the future."** **Kotler's Cadillac case is a healthy backward-looking metric concealing a dying forward-looking one** — the vault's *which metric?* theme in a new setting.

## 📘 Main Knowledge

### 1. What marketing is — and the five-step process

$$\textbf{marketing}=\text{engage customers, build relationships, create customer value}\ \textbf{in order to capture value in return}$$

> [!warning] ⚠️ The definition is a loop, and the last clause is the one students drop
> **Marketing is not philanthropy.** **Steps 1–4 are an investment; step 5 is the return.**

| step | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Understand** the marketplace and customer needs and wants |
| **2** | **Design** a customer value-driven marketing *strategy* — *who will we serve, and how?* |
| **3** | **Construct** an integrated marketing *program* that delivers superior value — **the 4Ps** |
| **4** | **Engage** customers, build profitable relationships, create customer delight |
| **5** | **⚠️ Capture value** from customers to create profits and **customer equity** |

**Steps 1–4 *create* value for the customer. Step 5 *captures* value from the customer.** **The first four are costs.**

### 2. Needs, wants, demands — the distinction exams test

| | |
|---|---|
| **needs** | states of felt deprivation — **not created by marketers** |
| **wants** | the form needs take **as shaped by culture and individual personality** |
| **demands** | wants **backed by buying power** |

*(Worked: **hunger** is a need — universal. **A Big Mac** is a want — cultural. **A Big Mac you can afford and will buy** is a demand.)*

> [!warning] ⚠️ This is the answer to "marketing creates needs"
> **It does not — needs pre-exist.** **Marketing shapes *wants* and converts them into *demands*.** **The criticism has force against wants and none against needs**, and knowing which is which is the whole of the reply.

**Market offering** = **some combination of products, services, information *or experiences*** offered to satisfy a need or want. **Not just physical goods.**

> [!warning] ⚠️ Marketing myopia
> **"The mistake of paying more attention to the *specific products* a company offers than to the *benefits and experiences* produced by those products."**
>
> **The classic test: a customer buying a quarter-inch drill bit wants a quarter-inch *hole*.** **A firm that defines itself by the bit misses the laser that makes holes without one.**
>
> **⚠️ Myopia is a *definition* error, not a *quality* error.** **A myopic firm can make an excellent product and still be destroyed, because it answered the wrong question about what business it is in.**

### 3. ⚠️ The five marketing management orientations

| orientation | the belief | what it does | focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **production** | consumers favour available and affordable products | improve production and distribution efficiency | inward |
| **product** | consumers favour quality, performance, innovation | keep improving the product | inward |
| **selling** | consumers won't buy enough unless pushed | large-scale selling and promotion | inward |
| **marketing** | know the needs and wants of target markets | deliver satisfaction better than competitors | **outward** |
| **societal marketing** | *also* consider consumers' **and society's long-run welfare** | balance profits, consumer wants, public interest | **outward+** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ Selling versus marketing is a matter of DIRECTION, not intensity
> | | starting point | means | ends |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **selling concept** | **the existing product** | selling and promoting | **profit through sales volume** |
> | **marketing concept** | **the customer** | integrated marketing | **profit through customer satisfaction** |
>
> **Kotler's gloss: the selling concept takes an *inside-out* view; the marketing concept an *outside-in* view.**
>
> **⚠️ So the selling concept is not a weaker marketing concept — it is the *opposite* one, running the causal arrow backwards.** **Which is why "we do a lot of marketing" usually means "we do a lot of selling".**
>
> *(And the **product** concept has its own failure mode, distinct from myopia: a firm can build an objectively better product that nobody wants, because "better" was defined by engineers rather than by customers.)*

### 4. ⚠️ Customer lifetime value — verified, then corrected

*(Kotler's Stew Leonard illustration, verified:)*

$$\$100\text{/week}\times50\text{ weeks}\times10\text{ years}=\mathbf{\$50{,}000}\ ✓$$

**And his point stands, and is the chapter's most useful idea:** **losing a customer is not losing *a sale* — it is losing *the whole stream*.** **That reframing is why "customer equity" exists as a concept.** *(He also cites Lexus at >\$600,000 and Starbucks at >\$14,000 in lifetime sales; both are stated without derivation and are not checkable.)*

> [!warning] ⚠️ But \$50,000 is not a *value*. Three things are wrong with it.
> **(1) It adds cash flows across ten years without discounting.**
>
> **[[Monetary and Financial Theories/contents/02 - The Meaning of Interest Rates|Monetary Theories ch. 02]] established the rule: *adding cash flows across time is not an operation you are allowed to perform*.** **There, a lottery advertising "\$20 million" was worth \$9.36m — 46.8% of the headline.** *(Here, computed:)*
>
> | discount rate | PV of \$5,000/yr for 10 yrs | % of \$50,000 |
> |---|---|---|
> | 0% | \$50,000 | 100.0% |
> | 5% | \$38,609 | 77.2% |
> | **8%** | **\$33,550** | **67.1%** |
> | 12% | \$28,251 | 56.5% |
>
> **The headline is only correct at a zero interest rate.**
>
> **(2) It is *revenue*, not profit.** **A customer who spends \$50,000 does not hand the grocer \$50,000 of value — the grocer keeps the margin.**
>
> | gross margin | lifetime gross profit | discounted at 8% |
> |---|---|---|
> | 10% | \$5,000 | \$3,355 |
> | **25%** *(typical supermarket)* | **\$12,500** | **\$8,388** |
>
> **(3) It assumes the customer stays ten years with certainty.** *(With an annual retention rate $r$, the expected discounted margin stream is $mM\cdot r/(1+i-r)$:)*
>
> | retention | CLV at 25% margin, 8% discount |
> |---|---|
> | 95% | \$9,135 |
> | 90% | \$6,250 |
> | **80%** | **\$3,571** |
>
> **⚠️ At 80% retention the customer is worth about \$3,600, not \$50,000 — the headline overstates by a factor of fourteen.**

> [!note] Is this an erratum? No — and the reason is worth recording
> **Kotler's *sentence* says Stew Leonard's "lost \$50,000 in lifetime **revenue**", which is exactly right.** **But the box is headed "customer lifetime **value**" and the definition given is "the **value** of the entire stream of purchases".**
>
> **⚠️ The word "value" is doing double duty — an accounting quantity in the definition and a revenue figure in the example — and the *V* in *CLV* means the discounted margin, not undiscounted turnover.**
>
> *(Rule 4 applied: the arithmetic is right, "revenue" is used correctly in the sentence carrying the number, and no convention makes \$50,000 a "value". **Not filed — recorded as a terminological gap.**)*

### 5. ⚠️ Capturing value — share of customer and customer equity

| | |
|---|---|
| **share of market** | your share of **all customers'** purchases in a category |
| **share of customer** | your share of **one customer's** category spending — *"share of wallet"* |

> [!warning] ⚠️ These are different growth strategies
> **Share of market means *acquire more customers*. Share of customer means *sell more to the ones you have*.**
> **And §4 says which is cheaper: acquiring a new customer costs multiples of retaining an existing one, and cross-selling needs no acquisition spend at all.**

**Customer equity** = **the total combined customer lifetime values of all current and potential customers.**

> [!warning] ⚠️ Kotler's sharpest line, and his best case
> **"Sales and market share reflect the *past*; customer equity suggests the *future*."**
>
> **The Cadillac case is the demonstration.** **Sales and market share looked healthy while customer lifetime value was falling — because the buyers were ageing.** **A healthy backward-looking metric concealed a dying forward-looking one**, and the response was to target a younger buyer.
>
> **⚠️ This is the vault's measurement theme in a new setting: *which metric?*** **Market share and customer equity can point in opposite directions, and nothing in either number announces the disagreement.** *(Compare [[Monetary and Financial Theories/contents/01 - The Financial System and What Money Is|Monetary Theories ch. 01]]'s "which M?" and [[Macroeconomics & Microeconomics/contents/08 - Measuring the Macroeconomy - GDP and the Cost of Living|Macro/Micro ch. 08]]'s GDP.)*

**The four relationship groups** *(profitability × projected loyalty)*:

| group | profitability | loyalty | **strategy** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **strangers** | low | low | **don't invest** — make money on every transaction |
| **butterflies** | **high** | low | **enjoy them now** — capture what you can, then stop |
| **true friends** | **high** | **high** | **invest continuously** — turn them into *true believers* |
| **barnacles** | low | **high** | sell more, raise fees, cut service — **or "fire" them** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ The counter-intuitive cell is *barnacles*: loyal and unprofitable
> **Kotler: "some loyal customers can be unprofitable, and some disloyal customers can be profitable."**
>
> **⇒ loyalty is not the objective — *profitable* loyalty is.** **A loyalty programme that recruits barnacles destroys value.**
>
> **And note the asymmetry in the advice: butterflies are to be exploited and released; barnacles are to be repaired or fired.** **⚠️ The only group worth relationship *investment* is true friends** — **so the framework is a budget-allocation rule disguised as a taxonomy.**

## ✏️ Exercises

**1. (Definitions.)** (a) Define marketing and give the five steps. (b) Distinguish needs, wants and demands. (c) Answer the charge that "marketing creates needs".

> [!example]- Solution
> **(a) A loop that ends in capture.**
>
> **"The process by which companies engage customers, build strong customer relationships, and create customer value *in order to capture value from customers in return*."**
>
> 1. **Understand** the marketplace and customer needs and wants
> 2. **Design** a customer value-driven **strategy** — who will we serve, and with what value proposition?
> 3. **Construct** an integrated marketing **program** — the 4Ps
> 4. **Engage** customers and build profitable relationships
> 5. **Capture value** — profits and customer equity
>
> **⚠️ Steps 1–4 create value *for* the customer; step 5 captures value *from* the customer.** **The first four are costs, and a firm that stops at step 4 has run a charity.** **Students routinely omit step 5 and thereby describe a different subject.**
>
> **(b) Deprivation → cultural form → purchasing power.**
>
> | | | example |
> |---|---|---|
> | **need** | a state of felt deprivation | hunger |
> | **want** | the form the need takes, **shaped by culture and personality** | a Big Mac |
> | **demand** | a want **backed by buying power** | a Big Mac you can afford and will buy |
>
> **The chain is one of increasing specificity, and each step adds something the previous lacked** — culture at the second, money at the third.
>
> **(c) It shapes wants; it does not create needs.**
>
> **Needs pre-exist marketing and are common across cultures — people were hungry before advertising.** **What marketing does is *shape the form* the need takes and *convert* the resulting want into a demand.**
>
> **⚠️ So the charge is misdirected as stated, and has real force when restated.** **"Marketing creates needs" is false. "Marketing shapes wants, sometimes toward things that serve the seller more than the buyer" is a serious criticism** — and it is the one the **societal marketing concept** (§3) exists to answer, by adding society's long-run welfare to the objective.
>
> *(Note the honest position: the framework anticipates the criticism rather than refuting it. Kotler devotes a whole later chapter to sustainable marketing — excluded from this scope per [[00-Index]], but its existence is the acknowledgement.)*

**2. (Hard — orientations.)** (a) Set out the five. (b) Why is selling *not* a weak form of marketing? (c) Classify three firms.

> [!example]- Solution
> **(a)** **Production** (available and affordable) → **product** (quality and innovation) → **selling** (push hard) → **marketing** (start from the customer) → **societal marketing** (add society's long-run welfare).
>
> **(b) Because the causal arrow runs the other way.**
>
> | | starts with | means | ends |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **selling** | **the existing product** | selling and promoting | profit through **volume** |
> | **marketing** | **the customer** | integrated marketing | profit through **satisfaction** |
>
> **Kotler: the selling concept takes an *inside-out* view; the marketing concept an *outside-in* view.**
>
> **⚠️ So selling is not less marketing — it is the opposite orientation.** **A firm doing intense selling is not doing "some" marketing; it is doing none, in this sense**, because it never asked what the customer wanted before building the product.
>
> **The practical diagnostic: ask what the firm did *first*.** **If the product existed before the customer research, it is a selling orientation however sophisticated the promotion.**
>
> **(c)**
> - **A utility with a regional monopoly cutting costs and expanding coverage** — **production concept**. Demand exceeds supply and the product is undifferentiated, so efficiency *is* the strategy. **Note this is not a criticism**: the production concept is correct in exactly those conditions.
> - **An engineering firm adding features nobody asked for** — **product concept**, and heading for **marketing myopia**. **The product may genuinely be better; "better" was just defined internally.**
> - **A timeshare operator with aggressive commissioned salespeople** — **selling concept**. **The tell is that the product is an *unsought good*** — one buyers do not normally think of buying — **which is exactly where the selling concept survives**, because there is no pre-existing want to discover.
>
> *(That last point is worth keeping: **the selling concept is not always wrong, it is wrong as a general philosophy.** For unsought goods and for excess inventory it is the appropriate response.)*

**3. (Hard — customer lifetime value.)** (a) Verify Kotler's figure. (b) What is wrong with it? (c) Compute it properly.

> [!example]- Solution
> **(a) \$50,000, and the reasoning is right.**
>
> $$\$100/\text{week}\times50\text{ weeks}\times10\text{ years}=\mathbf{\$50{,}000}\ ✓$$
>
> **The insight is genuine and is the chapter's best: losing a customer is not losing a sale, it is losing the entire stream.** **A shop that treats a complaint as a \$100 problem has mis-sized it by orders of magnitude** — which is why Stew Leonard's carves "Rule #1: the customer is always right" into a rock at the entrance.
>
> **(b) Three defects, and the first is one this vault has caught before.**
>
> **⚠️ (1) It adds cash flows across ten years without discounting** — **exactly [[Monetary and Financial Theories/contents/02 - The Meaning of Interest Rates|Monetary Theories ch. 02]]'s lottery error**, where "\$20 million" paid over twenty years was worth **\$9.36m, or 46.8% of the headline.** **A dollar in year 10 is not a dollar today.**
>
> **(2) It is revenue, not profit.** **The grocer keeps the margin, not the turnover.**
>
> **(3) It assumes ten years of patronage with certainty.** **Retention is probabilistic, and the assumed horizon is doing a great deal of work.**
>
> **(c) About \$3,600 — a factor of fourteen smaller.**
>
> | correction applied | value |
> |---|---|
> | headline | **\$50,000** |
> | + discounting at 8% | \$33,550 |
> | + 25% gross margin | **\$8,388** |
> | + 80% annual retention | **\$3,571** |
>
> **Using the standard infinite-horizon form $\text{CLV}=m\cdot M\cdot\dfrac{r}{1+i-r}$** with margin $m$, annual spend $M$, retention $r$, discount rate $i$.
>
> **⚠️ And note this is not a quibble — it changes decisions.** **A firm that believes a customer is worth \$50,000 will spend up to \$50,000 to acquire one.** **The correct number caps acquisition spend near \$3,600**, and the difference between those two budgets is the difference between a going concern and a bankruptcy.
>
> **Is it an erratum? No.** **Kotler's sentence says "lost \$50,000 in lifetime *revenue*", which is exactly correct.** **But the box is headed "customer lifetime *value*" and defines it as "the *value* of the entire stream".** **⚠️ The word "value" is doing double duty**, and *the V in CLV means the discounted margin*. **Recorded as a terminological gap, not filed.**

**4. (Capturing value.)** (a) Distinguish share of market from share of customer. (b) What is customer equity, and why does Cadillac matter? (c) What does this have in common with earlier subjects?

> [!example]- Solution
> **(a) Breadth versus depth.**
>
> **Share of market** is your slice of *all* customers' category spending; **share of customer** is your slice of *one* customer's.
>
> **⚠️ They are different growth strategies: acquire more customers, or sell more to existing ones.** **And §4's arithmetic favours the second** — acquiring a customer costs multiples of retaining one, and cross-selling carries no acquisition cost at all.
>
> *(The practical tools differ too: share of market is won by segmentation and positioning ([[07 - Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning|ch. 07]]); share of customer by product-line breadth and loyalty programmes ([[08 - Products, Services and Brands|ch. 08]]).)*
>
> **(b) The sum of all customers' lifetime values — and Cadillac shows why it matters.**
>
> **Kotler: "sales and market share reflect the *past*; customer equity suggests the *future*."**
>
> **⚠️ Cadillac's sales and market share looked healthy while its customer lifetime value was falling, because its buyers were ageing.** **A backward-looking metric concealed a dying forward-looking one**, and by the time sales reflected it the problem was a decade old. **The response — target younger buyers — was correct and expensive.**
>
> **The general lesson: a metric computed from *past transactions* cannot detect a deteriorating *customer base*, because the deterioration shows up in future transactions by construction.**
>
> **(c) ⚠️ It is the vault's *which metric?* theme, in a new setting.**
>
> **Market share and customer equity can point in opposite directions, and nothing in either number announces the disagreement.**
>
> **The same structure has now appeared repeatedly:** **[[Monetary and Financial Theories/contents/01 - The Financial System and What Money Is|Monetary Theories ch. 01]]** — *which M?*, where M1 and M2 gave inflation forecasts 8 points apart; **[[Macroeconomics & Microeconomics/contents/08 - Measuring the Macroeconomy - GDP and the Cost of Living|Macro/Micro ch. 08]]** — GDP and the CPI; **[[Monetary and Financial Theories/contents/04 - The Risk and Term Structure of Interest Rates|Monetary Theories ch. 04]]** — *which leg of the spread?*
>
> **⚠️ In every case the rule is the same: a number does not carry its own explanation, and a measure built for one question will silently answer a different one.** **Here the question is *past or future?*, and market share answers only the first.**

**5. (Hard — relationship groups.)** (a) Give the four. (b) Which cell is counter-intuitive and why? (c) What is the framework really for?

> [!example]- Solution
> **(a) Classified by profitability × projected loyalty.**
>
> | | low loyalty | high loyalty |
> |---|---|---|
> | **high profitability** | **butterflies** | **true friends** |
> | **low profitability** | **strangers** | **barnacles** |
>
> **Strangers**: don't invest — make money on each transaction. **Butterflies**: enjoy them while they last, capture what you can, then stop. **True friends**: invest continuously; turn them into *true believers* who tell others. **Barnacles**: sell more, raise fees, cut service — **or "fire" them.**
>
> **(b) ⚠️ Barnacles — loyal *and* unprofitable.**
>
> **Kotler: "some loyal customers can be unprofitable, and some disloyal customers can be profitable."**
>
> **⇒ loyalty is not the objective; *profitable* loyalty is.** **A loyalty programme measured on retention alone will happily recruit barnacles and destroy value while reporting success** — which is a measurement failure of exactly the kind (b) in exercise 4 describes.
>
> *(His example is small bank customers who bank regularly but do not cover the cost of maintaining their accounts. **They are loyal precisely because nobody else wants them**, which is worth noticing: in some markets loyalty is a *symptom* of unattractiveness to competitors.)*
>
> **(c) ⚠️ It is a budget-allocation rule disguised as a taxonomy.**
>
> **Read the four strategies together and only one of them involves spending on the relationship.** **Strangers get nothing, butterflies get transactional attention and then nothing, barnacles get repaired or fired.** **Only true friends receive investment.**
>
> **So the framework's output is not "here are four kinds of customer" — it is "spend your relationship budget on one quadrant".** **And that is the point of the chapter's title: relationship *management* means deciding where *not* to build relationships.**
>
> **⚠️ The honest caveat: the classification depends on *projected* loyalty and *potential* profitability, both of which are forecasts.** **Misclassify a true friend as a barnacle and you fire a profitable customer.** **The framework is only as good as the customer-level data behind it** — which is [[04 - Managing Marketing Information and Research|ch. 04]]'s subject, and in practice a prediction problem rather than a judgement.

## 📝 Summary

- **Marketing is a *loop*: engage, build relationships and create value **in order to capture value in return**.** **The last clause is the one students drop.**
- **Five steps: understand → design strategy → construct program (4Ps) → engage → **capture value**.** **The first four are costs.**
- **Needs (deprivation) → wants (culture) → demands (buying power).** **⚠️ Marketing shapes wants; it does not create needs** — which is the precise answer to a common criticism.
- **A market offering includes services, information and *experiences*, not just goods.**
- **⚠️ Marketing myopia is a *definition* error, not a quality error** — the drill bit versus the hole.
- **Five orientations: production, product, selling, marketing, societal.**
- **⚠️ Selling and marketing run the causal arrow in OPPOSITE directions** — inside-out versus outside-in. **Selling is not weak marketing; it is the other orientation.** **The diagnostic is what the firm did *first*.**
- **⚠️ Kotler's \$50,000 CLV verified** *(\$100 × 50 × 10)* — **and his insight is right: losing a customer loses the whole stream.**
- **⚠️ But it is REVENUE, not value** *(computed: **\$33,550** discounted at 8%; **\$8,388** at a 25% margin; **\$3,571** with 80% retention — **a factor of fourteen**)*.
- **⚠️ Defect (1) is [[Monetary and Financial Theories/contents/02 - The Meaning of Interest Rates|Monetary Theories ch. 02]]'s lottery error exactly** — adding cash flows across time.
- **⚠️ And it changes decisions**: believing \$50,000 justifies an acquisition budget fourteen times too large.
- **Not an erratum — Kotler's sentence says "lifetime *revenue*"** — **but the box says "value", and the *V* in CLV means the discounted margin.**
- **Share of market = more customers; share of customer = more per customer.** **Retention and cross-selling are cheaper than acquisition.**
- **Customer equity = the sum of all customers' lifetime values.** **⚠️ "Sales and market share reflect the past; customer equity suggests the future."**
- **⚠️ Cadillac: healthy market share concealing falling lifetime value, because buyers were ageing.** **A past-based metric cannot detect a deteriorating customer base.**
- **⚠️ The vault's *which metric?* theme again** — market share and customer equity can point opposite ways, and neither number announces it.
- **Four relationship groups: strangers, butterflies, true friends, barnacles.**
- **⚠️ Barnacles are loyal AND unprofitable ⇒ loyalty is not the objective, profitable loyalty is.**
- **⚠️ Only true friends get relationship investment — so the taxonomy is really a budget-allocation rule.**

## ⚠️ Important Notes

1. **⚠️ Marketing ends in *capturing* value.** A definition stopping at "creating value" describes a different activity.
2. **Steps 1–4 are expenditure.** The return is step 5.
3. **⚠️ Needs ≠ wants ≠ demands**, and the distinction decides whether a common criticism lands.
4. **Marketers shape wants, not needs.**
5. **A market offering can be an experience.** Do not restrict it to goods.
6. **⚠️ Myopia is about how the business is *defined*.** Excellent products do not protect against it.
7. **⚠️ Selling is the *opposite* of marketing, not a lesser version.** Inside-out versus outside-in.
8. **The production concept is correct when demand exceeds supply** — it is wrong only as a general philosophy.
9. **The selling concept survives for *unsought goods*.**
10. **⚠️ CLV means the DISCOUNTED MARGIN stream**, not undiscounted revenue.
11. **⚠️ Never add cash flows across years without discounting** — the vault's second instance.
12. **Revenue is not value.** Apply the margin before quoting a customer's worth.
13. **⚠️ Assumed retention does most of the work in a CLV figure.** State it.
14. **An inflated CLV inflates the acquisition budget** — the error is not academic.
15. **Share of market and share of customer are different strategies** with different tools.
16. **⚠️ Market share is backward-looking; customer equity is forward-looking.** They can disagree.
17. **⚠️ Loyalty is not the objective.** Barnacles are loyal and destroy value.
18. **Some customers should be fired.**
19. **⚠️ The four-group framework is a budget rule** — only true friends earn investment.
20. **It depends on *forecast* profitability and loyalty**, so it is only as good as the customer data.
