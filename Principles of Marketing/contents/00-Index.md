# Principles of Marketing — Map of Content

**Course framing.** **Marketing is not advertising.** **Kotler's own definition is the process by which companies *engage customers, build strong customer relationships, and create customer value in order to capture value from customers in return*** — so the subject is about the whole exchange, of which promotion is one of four instruments.

**For a Data Science reader specifically:** **this is the subject that supplies the *questions* the other subjects answer.** **Segmentation is clustering, A/B testing is hypothesis testing, churn and lifetime value are prediction problems, and attribution is causal inference.** **Kotler (2017) predates most of that, so those connections are added and flagged** *(see "Enrichment" below)*.

## Boundary with other subjects — decided, and recorded here

**This subject owns the *frameworks and the customer*.** **It does not own the economics of markets, the statistics of testing, or the machinery of prediction**, all of which the vault already holds:

- **[[Macroeconomics & Microeconomics/contents/02 - Supply, Demand and Elasticity|Macro/Micro ch. 02]] owns ELASTICITY** — including the finding that total revenue is maximised exactly where elasticity is 1 *(computed there: $P^*=3.5$, $TR^*=\$24.50$, $e=1.0000$)*. **Ch. 10 here uses elasticity; it does not re-derive it.**
- **⚠️ [[Macroeconomics & Microeconomics/contents/06 - Monopoly, Oligopoly and Monopolistic Competition|Macro/Micro ch. 05–06]] owns MARKET STRUCTURE — and this is the connection worth making loudly.** **Kotler's "differentiation" and "positioning" are, economically, what give a firm a *downward-sloping demand curve* and therefore pricing power.** **Kotler says differentiate and does not say why it pays; Mankiw says why it pays and does not call it positioning.**
- **[[Principle of Accounting/contents/00-Index|Principle of Accounting]] owns MARGINS AND CONTRIBUTION** — ch. 10's break-even arithmetic is cross-linked to it.
- **[[Mathematical Statistics/contents/07 - Hypothesis Testing - One Sample|Mathematical Statistics ch. 07]] owns HYPOTHESIS TESTING** — ch. 04's causal research is A/B testing, and the test is there.
- **[[Data Preparation and Visualization/contents/00-Index|Data Preparation and Visualization]] owns CLUSTERING AND DATA QUALITY** — ch. 07's segmentation is clustering, and ch. 04's "big data" problems are data-quality problems.
- **[[Econometrics/contents/00-Index|Econometrics]] owns CAUSAL INFERENCE** — ch. 12's attribution problem is an identification problem.

## ⚠️ Note shape — adapted, deliberately

**Kotler has almost no formulas, so the vault's standard template is adapted rather than padded** *(the reasoning is in this subject's `CLAUDE.md`)*:

- **`📘 Main Knowledge`** — **frameworks, named precisely**, with emphasis on what each one *distinguishes*.
- **`✏️ Exercises`** — still five, easy → hard, but as **applied cases and classification problems** with full model answers *("here is a firm and a situation — which strategy, and why not the other three?")*. **Where the material supports arithmetic — break-even, markup, margins, market share, customer lifetime value, marketing ROI — it is used and verified before being written down**, exactly as in every other subject.
- **`⚠️ Important Notes`** — **the highest-value section here, written as a disambiguation list.** **Marketing exams punish confusing adjacent terms**: segmentation vs targeting vs positioning, needs vs wants vs demands, the marketing concept vs the selling concept, advertising vs sales promotion vs publicity, product vs brand.

## ⚠️ Source and extraction

**Kotler & Armstrong, *Principles of Marketing* (Pearson 2017)** — the only file in `documents/`. **737 pages, 20 chapters in 4 parts, plus 3 appendices.**

- **⚠️ Extraction is CLEAN — the best in the vault so far.** **No operator cipher, no parenthesis fault.** **Formulas come through as readable text** *(`Markup percentage on cost = dollar markup / cost`)*, and body prose is unmangled.
- **Book page $n$ = PDF page $n+1$.**
- **⚠️ THE OUTLINE HAS A DEFECT — do not trust it blindly.** **It lists chapters 5 and 6 with the same title ("Consumer Markets and Buyer Behavior") and the same destination (PDF p. 157).** **Chapter 6 is actually *Business Markets and Business Buyer Behavior* and begins at PDF p. 187** *(verified from the running heads)*. **Every other entry checked out.**
- **Running heads have mangled capitalisation** (`parT 2`, `ChapTer 5`) — a font artifact, cosmetic, and confined to headers.
- **⚠️ All figures, framework diagrams and the many photographic ad examples are images and are lost.** **This is far less damaging here than in the diagram-driven subjects, because Kotler restates every framework in the prose** — but **the rule from [[Monetary and Financial Theories/contents/03 - The Behavior of Interest Rates|Monetary Theories ch. 03]] still applies: check whether the prose names a figure's content before recording it as lost.**

### Investigated and deliberately NOT filed

| Location | Apparent problem | Why not filed |
|---|---|---|
| **ch. 3, pp. 98–100** *(cohort definitions)* | **Millennials are "born between 1977 and 2000" and Gen Z "born after 1995"** — the two margin definitions **overlap by five years**, so the four cohorts as stated (78 + 49 + 83 + 82 = **292m**) double-count roughly 20 million people. | **Generational boundaries are genuinely disputed and no two sources agree.** **Kotler is *reporting* conventions, not deriving them, and he hedges Gen Z's start ("born after 1995 *in this group*").** **Rule 4 — the conventions ARE the disagreement, and filing would assert that one contested boundary is correct.** **⚠️ The durable warning: never sum cohort sizes across sources, and never treat a generational label as a partition.** → [[03 - Analyzing the Marketing Environment]] |
| **ch. 3, p. 97** *(boomer share)* | The 26% boomer population share implies a base of $78/0.26=$ **300 million** — the US figure around **2006**, not the book's 2017 vintage. | **Sourced statistics routinely lag their publication.** **A textbook citing a 2006-vintage percentage beside a later headcount is doing something ordinary, not wrong.** **Recorded because it is why the derived multiples should be read as orders of magnitude rather than decimals.** → [[03 - Analyzing the Marketing Environment]] |
| **ch. 1, p. 33** *(customer lifetime value)* | The box headed **"customer lifetime value"** defines it as *"the **value** of the entire stream of purchases"*, and the worked example produces **\$50,000 of undiscounted revenue** *(\$100 × 50 weeks × 10 years)*. | **The arithmetic is exact, and Kotler's own sentence correctly says "lifetime *revenue*".** *(Computed: discounting at 8% gives **\$33,550**; a 25% gross margin gives **\$8,388**; adding 80% annual retention gives **\$3,571** — **a factor of fourteen**.)* **So no convention makes \$50,000 a "value", but the number-bearing sentence is right.** **A terminological gap, not an error** — recorded because **a reader taking \$50,000 as a CLV will over-budget acquisition by an order of magnitude.** → [[01 - Marketing, Customer Value and Engagement]] |
