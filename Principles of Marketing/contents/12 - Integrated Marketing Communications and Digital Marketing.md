# Integrated Marketing Communications and Digital Marketing

**The last chapter of the subject, and of the vault. Four Kotler chapters, five results.**

**§7 — ⚠️ THE BOOK CONTRADICTS ITSELF ON THE ONE NUMBER A SALES-FORCE PLAN IS PROPORTIONAL TO.** **Appendix 2 puts available selling time at 1,250 of 2,000 hours — 62.5%. Ch. 16 says "actual selling time accounts for only 37 percent of total working time."** **68.9% apart** — and since the workload method gives $NS = NC\cdot FC\cdot LC/TA$, **the same method on the same book's two assumptions sizes HD's force at 80 reps or 135.** **⇒ 55 extra hires, \$2.76m a year, needing \$13.1m of extra sales to break even by [[10 - Pricing|ch. 10]]'s rule.**

**§4 — ⚠️ CPM CANNOT RANK MEDIA, AND HIS OWN NUMBERS PROVE IT.** **A Super Bowl spot costs \$45.05 per thousand; his banner-rate table runs \$25 to \$50.** **⇒ THE MOST EXPENSIVE TV INVENTORY ON EARTH IS CHEAPER PER THOUSAND THAN A "SPACE BANNER".** **What differs is attention per impression, not price per impression** — [[08 - Products, Services and Brands|ch. 08]]'s 0.20 seconds, in media buying.

**§8 — ⚠️ THE BUYER NOW SELF-SERVES FOUR OF HIS SEVEN SELLING STEPS.** *(Buyers complete "nearly 60 percent" — one source says "at least 57 percent" — of the buying process before contacting a salesperson, and $4/7 = 57.14\%$.)* **⇒ prospecting, preapproach, approach and presentation happen without the seller; the rep's job now begins at *handling objections*.**

**§10 — ⚠️ ONE DAY OF YOUTUBE UPLOADS WOULD TAKE 30,441 YEARS TO WATCH.** *(4 billion videos a day = 46,296 per second.)* **⇒ the constraint is attention, not impressions — the same finding as ch. 08's supermarket package, in a new medium.**

**§4 — ⚠️ ONE B-TO-B SALES CALL COSTS WHAT 13,319 TV IMPRESSIONS COST** *(\$600 against \$0.045)*, **which is why consumer marketers advertise and business marketers sell in person.** *[[06 - Business Markets and Business Buyer Behavior|Ch. 06]] priced.*

## 📘 Main Knowledge

### 1. The promotion mix and the communication process

> **Promotion mix (marketing communications mix)** — the specific blend of promotion tools the company uses to **persuasively communicate customer value and build customer relationships**.

| tool | | |
|---|---|---|
| **advertising** | any **paid**, **non-personal** presentation by an **identified sponsor** | reaches masses cheaply; **impersonal, one-way, expensive in total** |
| **sales promotion** | short-term **incentives** to encourage purchase or sale | attracts attention, **strong invitation to act now**; short-lived |
| **personal selling** | **personal** presentations by the sales force | **the most effective tool at later stages** — preference, conviction, action; **the most expensive** |
| **public relations** | building good relations by obtaining **favourable publicity** | **believable** — "seem more real and believable to readers than ads do"; underused |
| **direct and digital marketing** | engaging targeted individuals to obtain an **immediate response** | **immediate, customised, interactive** |

**The communication process has nine elements:** **sender, encoding, message, media, decoding, receiver, response, feedback, and noise.**

> [!note] ⚠️ Why the nine-element model earns its place
> **Two of the nine belong to the *receiver* (decoding, response) and one belongs to nobody (noise).** **⇒ so a campaign can be perfectly executed on every element the marketer controls and still fail**, which is the disciplined version of [[08 - Products, Services and Brands|ch. 08]]'s point that you choose a target and only *influence* a position.

**The buyer-readiness stages: awareness → knowledge → liking → preference → conviction → purchase.** **Advertising and PR do the early stages efficiently; personal selling does the late ones.**

### 2. ⚠️ Setting the promotion budget — four methods, one defensible

| method | what it does | Kotler's objection |
|---|---|---|
| **affordable** | set the budget at what the company **can afford** | **"completely ignores the effects of promotion on sales"**; makes annual planning impossible |
| **percentage-of-sales** | a fixed % of current or forecast sales | **⚠️ views sales as the CAUSE of promotion rather than the result**; gives no basis for choosing the percentage |
| **competitive-parity** | match competitors' spending | assumes competitors know better; no reason parity prevents wars |
| **objective-and-task** | define objectives, determine the tasks, estimate their costs, **sum them** | **"the most logical"** — it forces management to state assumptions |

> [!warning] ⚠️ The percentage-of-sales objection is the one to be able to state
> **"The percentage-of-sales budget is based on the availability of funds rather than on opportunities."** **⇒ it makes the budget *fall* exactly when sales fall — that is, when more promotion may be needed** — and it reverses cause and effect.
>
> **Objective-and-task is the only method that connects spend to a result.** *(His example: "a 95-percent-awareness level for its latest smartphone model during the six-month introductory period" — an objective you can cost and then check.)*
>
> **⚠️ And it is the only one that survives [[10 - Pricing|ch. 10]]'s discipline**: it names the outcome, so the spend can be tested against the contribution it must generate. **The other three are ways of deciding without deciding.**

### 3. Push, pull, and integration

| | |
|---|---|
| **push strategy** | push the product **through the channel** — the producer promotes to channel members, who promote to consumers *(personal selling and trade promotion)* |
| **pull strategy** | spend on **consumer advertising and promotion** to create demand; consumers ask channel members, who ask the producer |

**Most large firms use both.** *(Unilever spends \$8 billion worldwide each year.)*

> **Integrated marketing communications (IMC)** — carefully integrating and coordinating the company's many communications channels to deliver **a clear, consistent and compelling message** about the organization and its products.

> [!warning] ⚠️ IMC exists because the audience integrates whether the company does or not
> **"Consumers don't distinguish between message sources the way marketers do."** **A conflicting set of messages produces "blurred brand positions, diluted communications impact and confused positioning."**
>
> **⇒ so IMC is not a coordination nicety. The consumer builds one impression out of every contact, so the only question is whether the firm controls the blend.**

### 4. ⚠️ Advertising — and what CPM does and does not measure

**Four decisions** *(Figure 15.1)*: **objectives → budget → strategy (message and media) → evaluation.**

**An advertising objective is a specific communication task, classified by primary purpose:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **informative** | building **primary demand** — used heavily when a category is new |
| **persuasive** | building **selective demand**; includes **comparative advertising** |
| **reminder** | keeping relationships; **most important for mature products** |

*(These are [[09 - New Products and the Product Life Cycle|ch. 09]]'s life-cycle stages under another name: informative → introduction, persuasive → growth, reminder → maturity.)*

**The scale of the industry:**

| | |
|---|---|
| US measured advertising media | **~\$190 billion** |
| worldwide | **~\$545 billion** — the US is **34.9%** |
| P&G, the world's largest advertiser | **\$4.6bn US, \$11.5bn worldwide** |
| ⇒ P&G alone | **2.42% of US** and **2.11% of world** advertising |

**Media selection needs reach, frequency, impact and engagement, then media types, vehicles and timing.**

**⚠️ Kotler prints the cost figures that let CPM be computed, and never computes it.**

| | | **CPM** |
|---|---|---|
| **Super Bowl** *(\$5m per 30s, 111m viewers)* | | **\$45.05** |
| his banner rate card | standard banner | \$25 |
| | skyscraper | \$35 |
| | leaderboard | \$40 |
| | **space banner** | **\$50** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ THE MOST EXPENSIVE TELEVISION INVENTORY ON EARTH IS CHEAPER PER THOUSAND THAN A "SPACE BANNER"
> **A Super Bowl spot is 1.80× a standard banner and 0.90× a space banner.**
>
> **⇒ CPM CANNOT RANK MEDIA.** **If it could, no one would ever buy the Super Bowl, whose price is driven by simultaneity and cultural salience — neither of which CPM measures.**
>
> **⚠️ What differs across media is ATTENTION PER IMPRESSION, not price per impression** — which is exactly [[08 - Products, Services and Brands|ch. 08]]'s finding that a supermarket package gets 0.20 seconds. **An impression is not a unit of attention, and treating it as one is how media plans go wrong.**
>
> *(His other Super Bowl figure checks: "\$9 million for media time alone… \$150,000 per second" implies a **60-second** ad ✓. The two implied per-second rates — \$150,000 and \$166,667 — differ 11.1%, consistent with different years.)*

**And the price range within television is enormous:**

| | Super Bowl / this |
|---|---|
| average primetime 30s (\$112,100) | **44.60×** |
| Big Bang Theory (\$348,000) | 14.37× |
| Sunday Night Football (\$803,000) | **6.23×** |

**⚠️ Cost per CONTACT, across the whole promotion mix:**

| | |
|---|---|
| one TV impression at a \$45.05 CPM | **\$0.045** |
| one online / social / mobile contact | **\$25 – \$75** |
| **one B-to-B field sales call** | **~\$600** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ ONE SALES CALL COSTS WHAT 13,319 TV IMPRESSIONS COST, and a field call is 8–24× a digital contact
> **⇒ THAT is why consumer marketers advertise and business marketers sell in person.** **It is not a cultural difference between the two fields — it is the arithmetic of a market with a few large buyers against one with millions of small ones**, which is [[06 - Business Markets and Business Buyer Behavior|ch. 06]]'s opening distinction priced.

### 5. ⚠️ Production is a fixed cost — amortise it

*(His figures: **\$342,000** to produce a national 30-second TV ad; **\$112,100** for 30 seconds of time in a popular primetime programme.)*

| airings | total cost | **average cost per airing** |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | \$454,100 | **\$454,100** |
| 5 | \$902,500 | \$180,500 |
| **10** | \$1,463,000 | **\$146,300** |
| 50 | \$5,947,000 | \$118,940 |
| ∞ | — | **\$112,100** *(the asymptote)* |

> [!note] ⚠️ Production costs 3.05× one airing, and the average cost per airing falls 3.10× from 1 to 10
> **⇒ that is [[10 - Pricing|ch. 10]]'s fixed/variable split inside a media plan, and it explains two things at once:** why a single beautiful ad shown twice is a waste, **and why the marginal decision (buy one more airing at \$112,100) and the average decision (is this campaign worth \$454,100 a showing?) give opposite answers early in a flight.**
>
> **Always ask which cost a media argument is quoting.**

**Message strategy** turns the objective into "big ideas"; **execution styles** include slice of life, lifestyle, fantasy, mood, musical, personality symbol, technical expertise, scientific evidence and testimonial. **Format elements — size, colour, illustration — affect both impact and cost.**

**Evaluating advertising** means measuring **communication effects** (copy testing, before and after) and **sales and profit effects** — **and the latter is genuinely hard, because sales are also driven by price, product, availability and competition.** **⇒ return on advertising investment is Kotler's own stated open problem, not a solved metric.**

### 6. Public relations

**PR functions:** press relations, product publicity, public affairs, lobbying, investor relations, development. **Tools:** news, speeches, special events, written and audiovisual materials, corporate identity materials, and public service activities.

> [!note] ⚠️ Kotler's own argument for PR is an efficiency argument
> **"PR can be very effective and economical"** and is **more believable** than advertising, yet is **"often described as a marketing stepchild because of its sometimes limited and scattered use."**
>
> *(His illustration is measurable: the Chrysler/Ram "Farmer" campaign, run because **"traditional television advertising was out of reach budgetwise"**, drew **60 million YouTube hits in a month**, took subscribers **from 3,542 to 85,000 (+2,300%)**, and generated **>100 million views plus 50 million more in spoofs**.)*

> [!warning] ⚠️ But check a "media value" figure before quoting it
> **That campaign's media impact was "valued at more than \$170 million."** **Against its own ~100 million views that is \$1.70 per view; including spoofs, \$1.13.**
>
> **A bought Super Bowl impression costs \$0.045.** **⇒ THE VALUATION IMPLIES AN EARNED VIEW WAS WORTH 25× TO 38× A BOUGHT ONE.**
>
> **That may be defensible — a chosen, shared view really is worth more than an interrupted one — but it is an estimate, not a measurement, and the multiple is where the whole claim lives.** **Price earned media against what a bought impression costs before repeating the headline number.**

### 7. ⚠️ Personal selling — and the assumption the book states two ways

**Sales force management: designing strategy and structure → recruiting and selecting → training → compensating → supervising → evaluating.**

**Structures:** **territorial**, **product**, **customer (market)**, or **complex**. **Then size**, usually by the **workload approach**: group accounts by size and desired call frequency, sum the calls needed, and divide by the calls one rep can make.

$$NS = \frac{NC \times FC \times LC}{TA}$$

**⚠️ And here the book gives two different values for $TA$.**

| source | | |
|---|---|---|
| **[[10 - Pricing|Appendix 2]]** | 2,000 hours/year less 15 hours/week non-selling ⇒ **1,250 hours** | **62.5%** |
| **ch. 16** | *"actual selling time accounts for only 37 percent of total working time"* | **37%** ⇒ 740 hours |

**They are 68.9% apart, and $NS$ is proportional to $1/TA$:**

| HD's case *(2,500 outlets × 20 calls × 2 hours)* | **salespeople needed** |
|---|---|
| at Appendix 2's 1,250 hours | **80** |
| at ch. 16's 740 hours | **135** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ THE SAME METHOD, ON THE SAME BOOK'S OWN TWO ASSUMPTIONS, SIZES THE FORCE AT 80 OR 135
> **55 extra hires at \$50,000 = \$2.76 million a year of extra fixed cost** — and by [[10 - Pricing|ch. 10]]'s rule that needs **\$13.1 million of extra sales to break even**, against the \$4.76m Appendix 2 computes for its own 20 extra reps.
>
> **⇒ A SALES-FORCE PLAN IS PROPORTIONAL TO A NUMBER MOST FIRMS ASSUME AND FEW MEASURE.** *[[11 - Marketing Channels, Retailing and Wholesaling|Ch. 11]] noted that the workload method is a capacity calculation rather than an optimisation; this is why that matters.*
>
> **⚠️ And the same fact is an opportunity, not just a risk:**
>
> | raise selling time from 37% to… | selling capacity gained, no new hires |
> |---|---|
> | 45% | **+21.6%** |
> | 50% | +35.1% |
> | **74%** | **+100.0%** |
>
> **Doubling selling time doubles the sales force for free.** **⇒ that is why sales-force automation and administrative-load reduction are investments and not overhead.**

**Selection matters more than it looks:**

| | |
|---|---|
| top 30% of a typical sales force | **60% of sales** |
| **⇒ a top-30% rep vs a bottom-70% rep** | **3.50× as productive** |
| cost of a bad sales hire | **\$616,000** — **12.3 years of a \$50,000 salary** |
| Salesforce hires | **4.5% of applicants — 1 in 22** |

*(US firms spend ~\$20 billion a year on sales training; one programme returned **~340% ROI in 90 days**.)*

**Compensation combines a fixed amount, a variable amount, expenses and benefits — and the mix must match the selling job:** **a high variable share drives volume and a high fixed share protects relationship-building.**

### 8. ⚠️ The seven-step selling process — of which the buyer now does four

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. prospecting and qualifying** | identify **qualified** potential customers |
| **2. preapproach** | learn as much as possible **before** contact; set objectives; choose the approach |
| **3. approach** | meet, greet, and open the relationship |
| **4. presentation and demonstration** | tell the **value story**; the **customer-solution approach** beats a canned pitch |
| **5. handling objections** | seek out hidden objections; **treat them as opportunities** |
| **6. closing** | ask for the order |
| **7. follow-up** | ensure satisfaction and repeat business |

**⚠️ Now Kotler's own digital figures: 92% of business buyers start their searches online, and buyers complete "nearly 60 percent" — another source says "at least 57 percent" — of the buying process before contacting a salesperson.**

> [!warning] ⚠️ $4/7 = 57.14\%$. THE BUYER SELF-SERVES THE FIRST FOUR STEPS.
> **Prospecting, preapproach, approach and presentation now happen without the seller.** **⇒ the salesperson's job begins at *handling objections*, arriving at a buyer who already has a shortlist, a specification and a price expectation.**
>
> **That reframes every earlier step rather than deleting it:** **prospecting becomes *being findable*; preapproach becomes *publishing the information the buyer is searching for*; presentation becomes *content* rather than a meeting.** *(Hence 41% of outside sales activity now done remotely, and a 22% decline in B-to-B field roles.)*
>
> **⇒ AND IT IS [[06 - Business Markets and Business Buyer Behavior|CH. 06]]'S BUYING CENTRE, RESOLVED.** There the problem was that a seller cannot see who is deciding; here the buying centre does four steps in private and appears only at step five. **The two chapters describe the same shift from opposite sides, and Kotler connects them nowhere.**

**Transaction-oriented selling aims to close *a* sale; relationship marketing aims to build a long-term profitable relationship — and Kotler is explicit that "in the heat of closing sales, salespeople too" often forget the difference.**

### 9. Sales promotion

**Consumer tools:** samples, coupons, cash refunds (**rebates**), price packs, premiums, advertising specialties, patronage rewards, **point-of-purchase** displays, contests, sweepstakes and games. **Trade tools:** discounts, allowances, free goods, push money, specialty advertising. **Business tools:** conventions, trade shows, sales contests.

> [!warning] ⚠️ Sales promotion's defining property is also its defect
> **It offers a "strong invitation to act NOW" — and that is a short-term effect that decays.** **⇒ heavy promotion trains customers to wait for the next one** *([[10 - Pricing|ch. 10]]'s warning that a permanent promotion is a price cut with extra steps)*, **and by ch. 10's asymmetry a discount destroys profit far faster than it builds volume.**
>
> **Kotler's own framing is the right one: set promotion *objectives*, then choose tools, then develop and pretest, then implement, then EVALUATE** — and the evaluation step is the one that gets skipped.

### 10. ⚠️ Direct and digital marketing — and the attention constraint

> **Direct and digital marketing** — engaging directly with carefully targeted individual consumers and customer communities to **obtain an immediate response** and build lasting relationships.

**Forms:** **online marketing** (websites, online ads, email, online video, blogs); **social media marketing**; **mobile marketing**; and traditional **direct-response TV, catalogues, telemarketing, direct mail and kiosks.**

**Four content-marketing postures** — the firm may **create, inspire, share or shape** conversations. **And Kotler's honest warning is that shaping is the one nobody controls.**

**⚠️ The clutter figures, and what they mean.** *(His "daily diet": 500 million tweets, 4 billion YouTube videos, 58 million Instagram photos, 5 million Pinterest pins, 4.75 billion pieces of shared content — **per day**.)*

| | per day | **per second** |
|---|---|---|
| tweets | 500,000,000 | 5,787 |
| **YouTube videos** | **4,000,000,000** | **46,296** |
| shared content | 4,750,000,000 | 54,977 |

> [!warning] ⚠️ ONE DAY OF YOUTUBE UPLOADS WOULD TAKE 30,441 YEARS TO WATCH
> *(4 billion videos × 4 minutes.)*
>
> **⇒ THE BINDING CONSTRAINT IS ATTENTION, NOT IMPRESSIONS** — which is precisely [[08 - Products, Services and Brands|ch. 08]]'s finding that a supermarket package gets 0.20 seconds, arriving in a medium 3,000 miles away. **The same result in two settings is worth more than either.**
>
> **And it explains §4's CPM paradox.** **If impressions are effectively unlimited and attention is fixed, then price per impression converges across media while VALUE per impression does not** — so the Super Bowl's \$45 CPM and a banner's \$50 CPM buy radically different things at the same price.

**The advantages Kotler claims are real and checkable:** direct marketing is **immediate, low-cost, targeted and interactive**, and it is **measurable in a way mass media are not** — which connects it to [[04 - Managing Marketing Information and Research|ch. 04]]'s A/B testing and to the causal-inference problem that mass advertising cannot solve.

**Public policy issues:** irritation, unfairness, deception and fraud; **invasion of privacy**; and the special vulnerability of children. **CAN-SPAM and the Do Not Call registry.**

> [!note] ⚠️ The last idea in the book, and it closes the loop back to [[01 - Marketing, Customer Value and Engagement|ch. 01]]
> **Kotler ends where he began: the point of all of this is to build profitable customer relationships, and the communications tools are means.** **⇒ a campaign that maximises impressions, engagement or media value while failing to move contribution has optimised a proxy** — which is the single lesson every quantitative chapter of this subject has produced from a different direction.

> **⇒ the two chapters describe the same shift from opposite sides — one says the buyer is hard to observe, the other says the buyer has already decided most of it — and Kotler connects them nowhere.** **The practical consequence is that a seller's information advantage has inverted: the buyer now arrives better informed about the category than the rep is about the buyer.**

**5. (Clutter, earned media, and the close.)** (a) What do his daily-volume figures imply? (b) Check the "\$170 million" media value. (c) What is the closing lesson?

> [!example]- Solution
> **(a) ⚠️ One day of YouTube uploads would take 30,441 years to watch.**
>
> | | per day | per second |
> |---|---|---|
> | tweets | 500m | 5,787 |
> | **YouTube videos** | **4bn** | **46,296** |
> | shared content | 4.75bn | 54,977 |
>
> **⇒ THE BINDING CONSTRAINT IS ATTENTION, NOT IMPRESSIONS.**
>
> **That is [[08 - Products, Services and Brands|ch. 08]]'s 0.20-second package result in a completely different medium** — and two independent settings giving the same finding is worth more than either alone.
>
> **⚠️ And it explains exercise 2.** If impressions are effectively unlimited and attention is fixed, **price per impression converges across media while value per impression does not** — so a \$45 Super Bowl CPM and a \$50 banner CPM buy radically different things at nearly the same price.
>
> **(b) It implies an earned view was worth 25–38× a bought one — which is where the whole claim lives.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | \$170m ÷ 100m own views | **\$1.70 per view** |
> | \$170m ÷ 150m incl. spoofs | \$1.13 per view |
> | a bought Super Bowl impression | **\$0.045** |
>
> **The multiple may be defensible** — a chosen, shared, searched-for view really is worth more than an interrupted one — **but "media impact valued at" is an estimate, not a measurement, and a factor of 25–38 is a large thing to leave unstated.**
>
> **⇒ RULE: price earned media against what a bought impression costs, and quote the multiple, not the headline.** *(The campaign's real achievements are separately verifiable and impressive: subscribers from **3,542 to 85,000**, 60 million views in a month, all with a budget that could not afford television.)*
>
> **(c) That every metric in this chapter is a proxy, and the thing being proxied is contribution.**
>
> **Kotler ends where [[01 - Marketing, Customer Value and Engagement|ch. 01]] began — the point is profitable customer relationships, and communications are means.**
>
> **⚠️ And that is the single lesson this subject produced from a dozen directions:** [[01 - Marketing, Customer Value and Engagement|ch. 01]]'s customer lifetime value that was undiscounted revenue; [[02 - Company and Marketing Strategy|ch. 02]]'s share quoted without a growth rate; [[08 - Products, Services and Brands|ch. 08]]'s 98% quality standard that was an error count; [[09 - New Products and the Product Life Cycle|ch. 09]]'s launch that hit every volume target and lost \$50,000; [[10 - Pricing|ch. 10]]'s cost-plus price that was 33% below optimal; [[11 - Marketing Channels, Retailing and Wholesaling|ch. 11]]'s lower-margin retailer that earned more.
>
> **⇒ IN EVERY CASE A HEADLINE NUMBER WAS TRUE AND MEANT SOMETHING OTHER THAN WHAT IT APPEARED TO MEAN.** **The habit that catches all of them is the same one: take the source's own figures and divide them by each other.**

## 📝 Summary

- **Five promotion tools: advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, PR, direct and digital marketing.**
- **The communication process has nine elements; ⚠️ three lie outside the marketer's control** (decoding, response, noise).
- **Buyer readiness: awareness → knowledge → liking → preference → conviction → purchase.** Advertising does the early stages, selling the late.
- **Four budget methods; ⚠️ only objective-and-task connects spend to a result.**
- **⚠️ Percentage-of-sales treats sales as the CAUSE of promotion** and cuts the budget exactly when sales fall.
- **Push vs pull; IMC exists because ⚠️ the consumer integrates whether the firm does or not.**
- **Advertising objectives — informative / persuasive / reminder — are [[09 - New Products and the Product Life Cycle|ch. 09]]'s life-cycle stages renamed.**
- **US advertising ~\$190bn of a ~\$545bn world market (34.9%); P&G alone is 2.42% of the US.**
- **⚠️ SUPER BOWL CPM = \$45.05; his banner rates run \$25–\$50 ⇒ THE MOST EXPENSIVE TV ON EARTH IS CHEAPER PER THOUSAND THAN A SPACE BANNER.**
- **⇒ CPM CANNOT RANK MEDIA. What differs is attention per impression, not price per impression.**
- **⚠️ ONE B-TO-B SALES CALL (\$600) COSTS WHAT 13,319 TV IMPRESSIONS COST**, and 8–24× a digital contact.
- **⚠️ Ad production (\$342,000) is 3.05× one airing (\$112,100); average cost per airing falls 3.10× from 1 to 10 showings.**
- **PR is believable and cheap and Kotler calls it "a marketing stepchild."**
- **⚠️ A "\$170m media value" implies an earned view worth 25–38× a bought one — quote the multiple, not the headline.**
- **Sales force: territorial / product / customer / complex structures, then size by workload $NS = NC\cdot FC\cdot LC/TA$.**
- **⚠️ THE BOOK GIVES TWO VALUES FOR $TA$ — 62.5% and 37%, 68.9% APART — AND SIZES THE SAME FORCE AT 80 OR 135.** \$2.76m/yr, needing \$13.1m of sales.
- **⚠️ Raising selling time 37% → 74% doubles the sales force for free.**
- **⚠️ A top-30% rep is 3.50× a bottom-70% rep; a bad hire costs \$616,000 = 12.3 years of salary.**
- **The seven steps: prospecting → preapproach → approach → presentation → handling objections → closing → follow-up.**
- **⚠️ $4/7 = 57.14\%$ AND BUYERS COMPLETE 57–60% ALONE ⇒ THE BUYER SELF-SERVES THE FIRST FOUR STEPS.**
- **⇒ prospecting becomes being findable; the rep's job now begins at handling objections.** [[06 - Business Markets and Business Buyer Behavior|Ch. 06]]'s buying centre, from the other side.
- **Sales promotion invites action NOW — ⚠️ and trains customers to wait.**
- **⚠️ 4 BILLION YOUTUBE VIDEOS A DAY = 46,296 PER SECOND; ONE DAY WOULD TAKE 30,441 YEARS TO WATCH.**
- **⇒ attention is the constraint, impressions are not** — [[08 - Products, Services and Brands|ch. 08]]'s 0.20 seconds in a second setting.
- **⚠️ The closing lesson: every metric here is a proxy for contribution, and this subject found a true-but-misleading headline number in every single chapter.**

## ⚠️ Important Notes

1. **⚠️ Three of the nine communication elements are outside the marketer's control.**
2. **⚠️ Percentage-of-sales reverses cause and effect.** Never defend it.
3. **Objective-and-task is testable because it names an outcome.**
4. **Competitive-parity in a concentrated category produces an arms race** — three insurers, 46.5% of a \$6bn spend.
5. **⚠️ IMC is not tidiness; the consumer integrates regardless.**
6. **⚠️ CPM cannot rank media.** Say "cheapest per what."
7. **An impression is not a unit of attention.**
8. **⚠️ Compare cost per CONTACT across the whole mix before choosing tools.** \$0.045 / \$25–75 / \$600.
9. **⚠️ Ad production is a fixed cost — amortise it over the flight.**
10. **Marginal and average cost per airing give opposite answers early.** Ask which is quoted.
11. **Return on advertising investment is an open problem, not a metric.**
12. **⚠️ Price earned media against a bought impression and quote the multiple.**
13. **⚠️ Sales-force size is proportional to $1/TA$ — a number usually assumed, rarely measured.**
14. **⚠️ Raising selling time is cheaper than hiring.**
15. **A top-30% rep is 3.5× a bottom-70% rep** — selection dominates.
16. **⚠️ A bad sales hire costs 12 years of salary.**
17. **Compensation mix must match the job: variable drives volume, fixed protects relationships.**
18. **⚠️ The buyer completes 57–60% of the process alone — four of seven steps.**
19. **⇒ prospecting is now *being findable*, and presentation is now *content*.**
20. **The seller's information advantage has inverted.**
21. **⚠️ Sales promotion's urgency is also its decay** — and [[10 - Pricing|ch. 10]] says a discount destroys profit faster than it builds volume.
22. **Evaluate promotions. It is the step that gets skipped.**
23. **⚠️ Impressions are unlimited; attention is fixed.**
24. **⚠️ Every metric in this chapter is a proxy for contribution.** Check what moved.
