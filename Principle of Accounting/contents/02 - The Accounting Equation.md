# The Accounting Equation

[[01 - Introduction to Accounting]] noted that total assets equal total equity and liabilities. This chapter explains why, and the equation becomes the basis of double entry ([[03 - Accounting Transactions and Documents]], [[04 - Ledger Accounting and Double Entry]]).

## 📘 Main Knowledge

### Assets, liabilities, capital

**Assets** are resources the business owns that provide future benefits (cash, supplies, equipment).

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| non-current | for ongoing, long-term use | land and buildings, vehicles, machinery |
| current | for resale or realized in normal trading | inventory, receivables, cash |

Classification depends on purpose: a car is a non-current asset to a courier and inventory to a dealer.

**Liabilities** are claims against assets, debts owed to creditors: non-current if payable after more than 12 months (loans), current if within 12 months (trade payables, overdraft).

**Capital** is what the business owes back to the owner (business entity concept: the business is separate from its owner). For a sole trader:
$$C_{closing}=C_{opening}+\text{profit}+\text{capital injections}-\text{drawings}.$$
Profit belongs to the owner, so it raises capital; a loss lowers it. This links the profit statement to the balance sheet.

**Companies** have many owners who buy shares, so equity is split into **share capital** (paid in) and **retained earnings** (profits kept) plus reserves, and owners receive **dividends** instead of drawings. Company law restricts distributions, so the parts are kept separate. (The slides defer this to a "chapter 9" deck that doesn't exist.)

### The accounting equation

$$\text{Assets}=\text{Liabilities}+\text{Owner's equity}.$$
Assets are claimed by creditors or owners; in liquidation creditors are paid first, so equity is the **residual**: $\text{Equity}=\text{Assets}-\text{Liabilities}$. Because equity is defined as the residual, the equation always holds; if it doesn't balance, there is an error.

| Element | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| assets | resources owned, giving future benefits | cash, supplies, equipment |
| liabilities | claims against assets | trade payables, salaries payable |
| owner's equity | ownership claim, the residual | investments and revenues (+), drawings and expenses (−) |

**Expanded equation**:
$$\text{Assets}=\text{Liabilities}+\text{Capital}-\text{Drawings}+\text{Revenues}-\text{Expenses}.$$
- Increase equity: **investments** by the owner; **revenues** from earning activities (sales, fees, services, commissions, interest, dividends, royalties, rent).
- Decrease equity: **drawings** (cash or assets taken for personal use); **expenses** (assets consumed or services used to earn revenue: salaries, rent, utilities, tax).

| | Drawings | Expenses |
|---|---|---|
| purpose | owner's personal benefit | earning revenue |
| in profit or loss? | no | yes |
| effect on profit | none | reduces it |
| effect on capital | reduces it | reduces it through profit |

Likewise investments raise capital directly while revenues pass through profit.

### Linking the statements

Companies prepare a statement of financial position, a statement of profit or loss, a statement of cash flows and a statement of changes in equity. The balance sheet is the equation: non-current + current assets = capital + non-current + current liabilities, or net assets = assets − liabilities = capital. The profit statement: gross profit = revenue − cost of sales (the purchase or production cost of goods sold); profit = gross profit − expenses + non-trading income. Profit computed in the profit statement lands in capital on the balance sheet, so the two statements are one system.

## ✏️ Exercises

Exercises 1–4 are the lecture's (no answers given).

**1.** (DO IT!) Classify and state the effect on equity: rent expense; service revenue; drawings; salaries and wages expense.

> [!example]- Solution
> Rent: expense, decrease. Service revenue: revenue, increase. Drawings: drawings, decrease (not through profit). Salaries: expense, decrease.

**2.** (Question 1) The accounting equation can be rewritten as: A. assets + profit − drawings − liabilities = closing capital; B. assets − liabilities − drawings = opening capital + profit; C. assets − liabilities − opening capital + drawings = profit; D. assets − profit − drawings = closing capital − liabilities.

> [!example]- Solution
> **C.** $A-L=C_{close}=C_{open}+P-D$, so $A-L-C_{open}+D=P$. B has the wrong sign on drawings; A needs $P=D$; D implies $P+D=2L$. Quick check with numbers: A = 100, L = 40, $C_{open}$ = 50, D = 10 gives P = 20; only C works.

**3.** (Question 2) Profit for 20X7 was \$72,500; the owner injected \$8,000 and took goods costing \$2,200 for personal use; opening net assets were \$101,700. Closing net assets? A. 35,000; B. 39,400; C. 168,400; D. 180,000.

> [!example]- Solution
> **D.** Net assets = capital: $101{,}700+72{,}500+8{,}000-2{,}200=180{,}000$. Goods taken for personal use are drawings, at cost. The other options come from sign errors.

**4.** (Question 3) A sole trader borrows \$10,000 from a bank. Which elements change? A. assets and liabilities; B. assets and capital; C. capital and liabilities; D. assets only.

> [!example]- Solution
> **A.** Cash +10,000, loan +10,000; borrowing isn't income, so capital is unchanged. If the owner put in \$10,000, cash and capital would rise instead (B): same cash, different source.

**5.** Nguyen's consulting business: (1) invests £60,000 cash; (2) buys equipment £18,000 cash; (3) supplies on credit £3,000; (4) services for cash £24,000; (5) pays rent £5,000; (6) pays £2,000 to the supplier; (7) services on credit £9,000; (8) draws £4,000; (9) supplies used £1,100. Trace the equation and find profit.

> [!example]- Solution
> | # | Assets | Liabilities | Capital |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | cash +60,000 | | +60,000 (investment) |
> | 2 | cash −18,000, equipment +18,000 | | |
> | 3 | supplies +3,000 | payables +3,000 | |
> | 4 | cash +24,000 | | +24,000 (revenue) |
> | 5 | cash −5,000 | | −5,000 (expense) |
> | 6 | cash −2,000 | payables −2,000 | |
> | 7 | receivables +9,000 | | +9,000 (revenue) |
> | 8 | cash −4,000 | | −4,000 (drawings) |
> | 9 | supplies −1,100 | | −1,100 (expense) |
>
> Closing: cash 55,000 + receivables 9,000 + supplies 1,900 + equipment 18,000 = 83,900 = payables 1,000 + capital 82,900. Profit = 33,000 − 6,100 = £26,900; capital = 60,000 + 26,900 − 4,000 = 82,900.
> Buying equipment is an asset swap, not an expense; credit services are revenue (accruals); drawings aren't expenses (profit would otherwise show 22,900); supplies become an expense when used ([[05 - Adjusting Entries]]); the equation balances after every line.

## 📝 Summary

- Assets (non-current/current by purpose) and liabilities (by 12-month horizon).
- Capital = what the business owes the owner; $C_{close}=C_{open}+P+\text{injections}-D$.
- Companies split equity into share capital and retained earnings; dividends replace drawings.
- Assets = liabilities + equity; equity is the residual, so the equation always holds.
- Revenues and investments raise equity; expenses and drawings lower it; only revenues and expenses pass through profit.
- Profit links the two main statements.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

1. Drawings are not expenses (goods taken, the owner's personal bills, "wages" to a sole trader are all drawings).
2. Buying an asset or repaying a liability is not an expense.
3. Borrowing is not income.
4. Net assets and capital are the same number.
5. Every transaction has at least two effects (asset swap; asset and liability up or down; asset and capital up or down; one liability for another), which double entry formalizes.
6. For rearrangement questions, test options with simple numbers.
