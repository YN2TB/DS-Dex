(A flat-bottomed U is almost the *opposite* of what "U-shaped" implies.) **Deregulation (Riegle-Neal 1994) is the explanation with the cleanest evidence** — implying the pre-1994 structure was an artefact of law, not efficient scale. Also derived $\sigma_{comb}=\sigma\sqrt{(1+\rho)/2}$: **0.0% risk reduction at ρ=1, 2.5% at ρ=0.9** — which is *why* R&H's cited Levonian/Rose finding (crossing state lines ≠ diversification) holds.

- **Two ch. 05 traps worth reusing:** (i) **$\Delta NII = \text{gap}\times\Delta i$ is EXACT, not approximate** — verified against direct recomputation at every shock; the approximation lives in the *classification* (bucket timing, demand-deposit sensitivity, non-parallel shifts), not the arithmetic. (ii) **the duration gap is $D_A - u D_L$ with $u = L/A$** — **omitting $u$ always makes the bank look better hedged than it is** and is the standard exam error.
- **Ch. 05's framing to carry:** **perfect immunisation abolishes the business model.** Quantified — reaching $D_{gap}=0$ needs liabilities lengthened **1.40 → 3.55 yrs** or assets shortened **3.20 → 1.26 yrs**, i.e. removing the maturity mismatch that *is* the spread (ch. 01 §4). **So banks set a tolerance, hedge the excess with derivatives (ch. 06 — a swap changes exposure without touching the loan book), and hold capital against the rest (ch. 10).** Same pattern as ch. 01: risk is priced and controlled, never eliminated.
- **Ch. 06 — the subject's best result so far, and it is mine: tranching is a bet on CORRELATION, not credit quality.** One-factor simulation, 1,000 loans at $p=5\%$, tranches 0–5/5–20/20–100: **mean pool loss is 5.00% at EVERY correlation** while the **senior tranche's expected loss goes 0.0000% (ρ=0) → 1.8044% (ρ=0.6)** and $P(\text{pool loss}>20\%)$ goes 0.0000% → 7.6380%. **Correlation moves no loss into the pool — only up the capital structure. Nothing about the loans had to change for the AAA to be wrong.** R&H describes tranching and says ratings were "often not carefully done" but gives **no model and no number**.

- **⚠️ Ch. 11's best result is R&H's OWN idea, taken out of a footnote and computed** (footnote 6, p. 579: charging the full risk premium "may INCREASE the chances a borrower will default"). **Modelled with $p(r)=p_0+k(r-r_0)$: the expected return is HUMPED, peaking at $r^*=18.00\%$ with a 14.20% default probability; at 34% the return is 4.11%, WORSE than the 5.34% available at 6%.** **Beyond $r^*$ the lender earns less by charging more, because the rate itself creates the default** ⇒ the correct response is **REFUSAL, not repricing** — credit rationing, which is why loan markets don't clear like ordinary markets. **The six Cs are the screen this requires: Character and Capacity are GATES, not dials.** *(This is Stiglitz–Weiss; R&H gestures at it without naming it.
