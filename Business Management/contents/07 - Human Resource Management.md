## 📘 Main Knowledge

### 1. What HRM is, and why it stopped being clerical

> **Human resource management (HRM)** — the process of **determining human resource needs** and then **recruiting, selecting, developing, motivating, evaluating, compensating and scheduling** employees to achieve organizational goals.

> [!warning] ⚠️ Nickels names the two forces that moved HRM from the back office to the top table
> **(1) organizations' recognition of employees as their ultimate resource, and (2) the changing nature of work itself.**
>
> **⇒ and [[01 - Business, Profit and Risk|ch. 01]] supplies the theory: KNOWLEDGE is the fifth factor of production and, per Drucker, "the most important."** **A firm whose scarce asset is knowledge has its balance sheet walking out of the building every evening** — which is why the function that manages those people cannot be a records department.
>
> *(His Chobani opener makes the same point with equity: staff shares are **about 10% of a company valued at \$3 billion**, with **"the average employee's stake worth as much as \$150,000."** **⇒ 10% of \$3bn is \$300 million, which at \$150,000 each implies exactly 2,000 employees — an internal check that passes** — and against **over \$1.5 billion** of revenue that is **\$750,000 of revenue per employee.**)*

**Current challenges he names:** a shortage of trained workers in growth areas; a surplus of unskilled workers; **increasing numbers of skilled and unskilled workers from declining industries**; a shortage of workers in skilled trades; an increasingly diverse population; a **shift in employee attitudes** — *"leisure time has become a much higher priority"*; and complex, changing laws.

### 2. ⚠️ Five steps in HR planning

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Prepare a human resource INVENTORY** — ages, names, education, capabilities, training, specialized skills, languages. *"It reveals whether the labor force is technically up to date and thoroughly trained."* |
| **2** | **Prepare a JOB ANALYSIS** — a study of what employees holding various job titles actually do |
| **3** | **Assess future human resource DEMAND** |
| **4** | **Assess future SUPPLY** |
| **5** | **Establish a strategic PLAN** — recruiting, selecting, training, developing, appraising, compensating, scheduling |

**Job analysis produces two documents, and confusing them is the classic error:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **job description** | the objectives of the job, type of work, responsibilities and duties, working conditions, and its relation to other functions |
| **job specifications** | a written summary of the **minimum qualifications** a person needs to do it |

> [!warning] ⚠️ Nickels's own one-line test is the thing to memorise
> **"Job descriptions are about the JOB, and job specifications are about the PERSON who does the job."**
>
> **⇒ and the order matters: the description must come first.** **A specification written before a description is a description of the person you already had in mind** — which is how job requirements come to include qualifications the work does not need, and how §3's discrimination exposure is created.

### 3. ⚠️ Employment law — and the thresholds nobody names

**The legislation, in date order, with what each did:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **National Labor Relations Act 1935** | established **collective bargaining** |
| **Fair Labor Standards Act 1938** | **minimum wage** and **overtime beyond 40 hours** |
| **Equal Pay Act 1963** | men and women doing **equal jobs** must be **paid the same wage** |
| **Civil Rights Act 1964 (Title VII)** | for firms with **15 or more employees**, outlawed discrimination based on race, religion, creed, sex or national origin *(**amended 97 times before passage**)* |
| **Age Discrimination in Employment Act 1967** | applies to employers with **20 or more employees** |
| **Occupational Safety and Health Act 1970** | safety and health conditions in most private industry |
| **Equal Employment Opportunity Act 1972** | strengthened the **EEOC** |
| **Vocational Rehabilitation Act 1973** | extended protection to **any physical or mental disability** |
| **ERISA 1974** | regulated and insured **company retirement plans** |
| **Immigration Reform and Control Act 1986** | employers must **verify employment eligibility** of all new hires |
| **Americans with Disabilities Act 1990** | employers must make **reasonable accommodation** |
| **Civil Rights Act 1991** | for firms **over 15 employees**, extends the right to a **jury trial and punitive damages** |
| **Family and Medical Leave Act 1993** | businesses with **50 or more employees** must provide up to **12 weeks** of leave |
| **ADA Amendments Act 2008** | broader protection for workers with disabilities |
| **Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act 2009** | changed when the **180-day filing clock** starts |

> [!warning] ⚠️ THREE OF THESE HAVE EMPLOYEE-COUNT THRESHOLDS, AND NICKELS NEVER SAYS THEY ARE THRESHOLDS
> | at… | the firm becomes subject to |
> |---|---|
> | **15 employees** | Civil Rights Act 1964 and 1991 — discrimination liability, jury trial, punitive damages |
> | **20 employees** | Age Discrimination in Employment Act |
> | **50 employees** | Family and Medical Leave Act — up to 12 weeks of leave |
>
> **⇒ A FIRM AT 49 EMPLOYEES AND ONE AT 50 FACE MATERIALLY DIFFERENT LAW.**
>
> **The compliance burden is a STEP FUNCTION, not a smooth cost of growing** — and every step lands exactly where a small firm is deciding whether to hire one more person. **⇒ so the marginal cost of the 15th, 20th and 50th employee is not their salary; it is their salary plus an entire regulatory regime.**
>
> *(This is the same structural insight as [[04 - Ethics and Corporate Social Responsibility|ch. 04]]'s whistleblower threshold: **a rule with a cut-off creates a discontinuity, and behaviour bunches just below it.**)*

> [!note] ⚠️ And one honest complication Nickels raises
> **The Civil Rights Act of 1964 "was expected to stamp out discrimination in the workplace"** — and he goes on to discuss **affirmative action** and **reverse discrimination**, noting the courts have found both. **⇒ a law can create a second problem while solving the first, and the chapter does not pretend otherwise.**

### 4. Recruiting — and why it is hard

> **Recruitment** — the set of activities for obtaining the **right number of qualified people at the right time**, to select those who best meet the organization's needs.

| **internal sources** | **external sources** |
|---|---|
| transfers, promotions, employee recommendations | advertisements, public and private employment agencies, **college placement**, management consultants, professional organizations, referrals, online sites, **walk-in applications** |

**Why it is difficult:** legal restrictions make hiring and firing decisions consequential; **some organizations have unattractive images**; and **internal sources are often insufficient**, so external recruiting is unavoidable. *(He reports **45% of CEOs surveyed** find finding talent challenging.)*

**Contingent workers** — those who do not have the expectation of regular, full-time employment: part-time, temporary, seasonal, independent contractors, interns, co-op students. *(He cites approximately **5.7 million** contingent workers in the US and an estimate that **by 2019 roughly 50%** of the workforce could be contingent.)*

> [!note] ⚠️ Contingent work shifts risk, and both sides know it
> **Firms use contingent workers when demand varies, when full-time workers are on leave, or in a downturn.** **⇒ the firm converts a fixed cost into a variable one, and the worker absorbs the variance** — which is exactly the fixed-versus-variable distinction [[Principles of Marketing/contents/10 - Pricing|Marketing ch. 10]] showed governs operating leverage.
>
> *(And a firm reporting **100,000 employees and 100,000 subcontractors** has half its workforce off its own books.)*

### 5. ⚠️ Selection — six steps, and what they cost

> **Selection** — the process of **gathering information and deciding who should be hired**, under legal guidelines, to serve the best interests of the individual and the organization.

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Obtaining complete application forms** — *equal employment laws limit the questions* |
| **2** | **Conducting initial and follow-up interviews** — HR screens, then the hiring manager and prospective colleagues |
| **3** | **Giving employment tests** — competency, personality, interests; **must be directly related to the job** |
| **4** | **Conducting background investigations** |
| **5** | **Obtaining results from physical exams** |
| **6** | **Establishing trial (probationary) periods** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ Step 2 carries a legal trap Nickels states explicitly, and it is worth quoting
> **"No matter how innocent the intention, missteps such as asking about pregnancy or child care could later be evidence if the applicant files discrimination charges."**
>
> **⇒ the interviewer's INTENT is irrelevant to the evidentiary value of the question.** **That is why step 3's rule — tests "should always be directly related to the job" — is the same rule stated for a different instrument: relevance to the job is the only defence for any selection criterion.**
>
> **And §2's ordering explains why: if job specifications are written from the description rather than from a person, every question has an obvious job-related justification.**

**⚠️ And here is the cost, which is the chapter's most useful number.** *"It can cost **75 percent of the employee's annual salary** to recruit, process, and train even an entry-level worker, and **more than 200 percent** a year's salary for a top manager."*

| salary | **entry-level (75%)** | **top manager (200%)** |
|---|---|---|
| \$30,000 | \$22,500 | \$60,000 |
| **\$50,000** | **\$37,500** | **\$100,000** |
| \$80,000 | \$60,000 | \$160,000 |

> [!warning] ⚠️ THIS IS THE THIRD INDEPENDENT FIGURE ON THE SAME QUESTION, AND IT SHARPENS THE RESULT TENFOLD
> **[[Principles of Marketing/contents/08 - Products, Services and Brands|Marketing ch. 08]] computed that the Four Seasons' six free room-nights per employee break even at a \$2,500 replacement cost** *(turnover 18% against an industry 36%, the room costed at marginal cost)*.
>
> | source | figure on a \$50,000 salary | clears the break-even by |
> |---|---|---|
> | [[04 - Ethics and Corporate Social Responsibility|ch. 04]]'s floor (6%) | \$3,000 | **1.2×** |
> | **ch. 07 entry-level (75%)** | **\$37,500** | **15×** |
> | ch. 07 top manager (200%) | \$100,000 | **40×** |
>
> **⇒ THREE INDEPENDENT FIGURES, ONE CONCLUSION — and the most SPECIFIC of them turns a marginal result into an overwhelming one.**
>
> **⚠️ But the two chapters disagree at the floor: ch. 04 said 6–213%, ch. 07 says 75–200%+. The floors are 12.5× apart.** **Not filed — different studies, and ch. 07 names what its figure *includes* ("recruit, process, and train") while ch. 04 does not.** *Recorded in the errata table.*

### 6. Training and development

> **Training and development** — all attempts to improve productivity by **increasing an employee's ability to perform**. *Training focuses on **short-term** skills; development on **long-term** abilities.*

| method | |
|---|---|
| **orientation** | initiates new employees into the organization, colleagues, supervisors and policies |
| **on-the-job training** | learn by doing, or by watching others |
| **apprenticeship** | a period of learning under a skilled worker |
| **off-the-job training** | away from the workplace — often conceptual |
| **online training** | **"programs available 24 hours a day"** and systems that monitor progress |
| **vestibule training** | in classrooms with equipment **similar to that on the job** |
| **job simulation** | duplicates the **exact** conditions of the job *(pilots, astronauts)* |

**Management development** adds: **on-the-job coaching**, understudy positions, **job rotation**, and off-the-job courses and training.

| | |
|---|---|
| **networking** | establishing and maintaining contacts with key managers in your own and other organizations, weaving relationships that serve as **informal development systems** |
| **mentor** | an experienced employee who **supervises, coaches and guides** lower-level employees, **introducing them to the right people** and being their **organizational sponsor** |

> [!note] ⚠️ Vestibule training and job simulation differ in one word, and it is the expensive word
> **Vestibule uses equipment *similar to* the job's; simulation duplicates the *exact* conditions.** **⇒ the cost gap between them is the cost of exactness, and it is justified precisely where a mistake on the real job is catastrophic** — which is why the examples are pilots and astronauts and not retail clerks.

### 7. ⚠️ Appraisal — which is the control process again

> **Performance appraisal** — an evaluation that **measures employee performance against established standards** in order to make decisions about **promotions, compensation, training or termination**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Establishing performance standards** — *"crucial. Standards must be understandable, subject to measurement, and reasonable. Both manager and subordinate must accept them."* |
| **2** | **Communicating those standards** — *"it's dangerous to assume that employees know what is expected of them"* |
| **3** | **Evaluating performance** — *"if the first two steps are done correctly, this is relatively easy"* |
| **4** | **Discussing results** with employees |
| **5** | **Taking corrective action** |
| **6** | **Using the results to make decisions** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ THIS IS [[06 - Management and Leadership|CH. 06]]'S FIVE-STEP CONTROL PROCESS WITH A PERSON AS THE OBJECT
> | **ch. 07 — appraisal** | **ch. 06 — control** |
> |---|---|
> | establish performance standards | establish clear performance standards |
> | communicate those standards | *(implicit)* |
> | evaluate performance | monitor, record, compare against standards |
> | discuss results with employees | communicate results and deviations |
> | take corrective action | take corrective action |
> | use results to make decisions | *(feedback: are standards realistic?)* |
>
> **And step 1 is almost verbatim in both:** ch. 06 requires standards that are *"specific, attainable, and measurable"*; ch. 07 requires them *"understandable, subject to measurement, and reasonable."*
>
> **⇒ Nickels presents these 100 book-pages apart as unrelated topics.** **Seeing them as one process is worth more than either list**, because everything the vault knows about the control function then applies to appraisal — including **[[Principles of Marketing/contents/10 - Pricing|Marketing ch. 10]]'s variance decomposition, where the most visible cause explained only 48.1% of a miss.** **An appraisal that attributes a shortfall to the most visible cause is making exactly that error about a person.**
>
> **⚠️ And ch. 07 adds one requirement ch. 06 lacks: "BOTH MANAGER AND SUBORDINATE MUST ACCEPT" the standards.** **⇒ a control system needs measurable standards; an appraisal system needs measurable AND agreed ones**, because the object of measurement is a person who can dispute them.

**360-degree review** — gathering opinions from **all around** the employee: above, below and alongside.

### 8. Compensation and benefits

**Compensation programs have five objectives:** attract the people needed · **provide an incentive to work efficiently** · keep valued employees from leaving · maintain a **competitive position** in the market · **provide employees with financial security**.

| pay system | |
|---|---|
| **salary** | fixed compensation regularly paid |
| **hourly wage / daywork** | a specified rate per hour |
| **piecework** | by the **number of items produced** rather than by time — *"creates powerful incentives to work efficiently"* |
| **commission plans** | pay based on **sales** |
| **bonus plans** | extra pay for **accomplishing or surpassing objectives** — monetary or cashless |
| **profit-sharing** | annual bonuses based on the **company's profits** |
| **gain-sharing** | bonuses based on **achieving goals related to productivity, quality or cost** |
| **stock options** | the right to buy stock at a **specific price over a specific period** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ The pay systems differ in what they make measurable, not in generosity
> **Piecework measures OUTPUT; commission measures SALES; gain-sharing measures PRODUCTIVITY, QUALITY OR COST; profit-sharing measures the FIRM.** **⇒ each moves the incentive to whatever it can count** — and each therefore creates the distortion of whatever it cannot.
>
> **This is [[Principles of Marketing/contents/12 - Integrated Marketing Communications and Digital Marketing|Marketing ch. 12]]'s compensation point restated: a high variable share drives volume, a high fixed share protects relationship-building.** **The choice is not how much to pay but WHICH NUMBER TO ATTACH THE PAY TO.**
>
> *(For teams, he names **gain-sharing** and **skill-based pay** as the two most common — the first measures the team's result, the second the member's growth.)*

**⚠️ Stock options, with his own worked figures:** *"He can use those options to buy the 10,000 shares (now worth \$1 million) for \$100,000."*

| | |
|---|---|
| current price | **\$100/share** |
| strike price | **\$10/share** |
| **gain** | **10× — a profit of \$900,000** |

**Fringe benefits** — sick-leave pay, vacation pay, pension and health plans, and *"anything else of value"*.

> [!warning] ⚠️ Benefits went from under 2% of payroll to over 30% — a 15-fold rise in their share
> **"In 1929, fringe benefits accounted for less than 2 percent of payroll; today they can account for over 30 percent."**
>
> **⇒ so a firm quoting a salary is quoting roughly three-quarters of what the employee costs it** — and [[05 - How to Form a Business|ch. 05]] notes the same 30%+ figure from the employer's side. **This also means §5's replacement costs, expressed as a percentage of SALARY, understate the true loss**, because the outlay being replaced is about 130% of salary.

| | |
|---|---|
| **cafeteria-style benefits** | employees **choose the benefits they want** up to a certain dollar amount |
| **soft benefits** | help maintain the **work–family balance** — *he notes two newer ones aimed at Millennials: **help paying off student loan debt** and **low-cost loans***|

### 9. Scheduling, and moving through the company

| | |
|---|---|
| **flextime plan** | freedom to choose **which hours to work**, provided the required hours are worked — built around **core time** *(e.g. 9–11 a.m. and 2–4 p.m.)* when all must be present |
| **compressed workweek** | the **full hours in fewer days** *(four 10-hour days)* |
| **home-based work** | **24% of US workers now work from home at least once a week**, and **68% expect to work remotely in the future** |
| **job sharing** | two part-time employees split one full-time job |

> [!warning] ⚠️ The 68% is a STATED EXPECTATION, and the vault has measured that gap
> **68% against a current 24% is 2.83× — but [[Principles of Marketing/contents/09 - New Products and the Product Life Cycle|Marketing ch. 09]] measured stated-versus-actual at 2.41×.**
>
> **⇒ deflated, 68% becomes about 28.2% — barely above today's 24%.**
>
> **The same deflator applied to [[04 - Ethics and Corporate Social Responsibility|ch. 04]]'s "50% will pay more for socially responsible goods" gave ~20.7%.** **⇒ a stated intention is not a forecast, and this is the second time in this subject that treating one as a forecast would mislead.**
>
> *(His flexibility demand figures are stronger evidence because they are about the past, not the future: **76% of Gen Xers say they look for jobs allowing flexible schedules** and **66% of Millennials say they LEFT jobs that didn't** — a report of behaviour rather than intent.)*

**Moving through the company:** **promotion** *(and lateral **reassignment**)*, **termination**, and **retirement**. *He notes the trend to **promote from within** as an inexpensive way to improve morale, and that firms often prefer **early retirement offers** to layoffs.*

> **⇒ a firm quoting a salary is quoting roughly three-quarters of what the employee costs it.** **And it means §5's replacement costs, expressed as a percentage of SALARY, understate the true loss by about a third**, because the outlay being replaced is ~130% of salary.
>
> *(**Cafeteria-style** benefits let employees choose up to a dollar amount; **soft benefits** target work–family balance, and the two newest are **student-loan repayment help** and **low-cost loans**.)*
>
> **(c) ⚠️ The 68% is a stated EXPECTATION, and it deflates to about 28.2%.**
>
> **24% work from home at least weekly now; 68% expect to in future — 2.83×.** **But [[Principles of Marketing/contents/09 - New Products and the Product Life Cycle|Marketing ch. 09]] measured stated-versus-actual intent at 2.41×**, giving **~28.2% — barely above today's 24%.**
>
> **⇒ this is the SECOND time in this subject that a stated intention would mislead if read as a forecast** *(the first was [[04 - Ethics and Corporate Social Responsibility|ch. 04]]'s "50% will pay more", which deflated to ~20.7%)*.
>
> **⚠️ His flexibility figures are better evidence, because they report BEHAVIOUR rather than intent:** **76% of Gen Xers say they look for jobs allowing flexible schedules**, and **66% of Millennials say they LEFT jobs that didn't.** **The second is a revealed preference and carries far more weight than the first.**

## 📝 Summary

- **HRM = determining needs, then recruiting, selecting, developing, motivating, evaluating, compensating and scheduling.**
- **⚠️ It rose in importance because employees are the ultimate resource** — [[01 - Business, Profit and Risk|ch. 01]]'s knowledge as the fifth factor of production.
- **Chobani: 10% of \$3bn ÷ \$150,000 each ⇒ exactly 2,000 employees — an internal check that passes.**
- **Five planning steps: inventory → job analysis → future demand → future supply → strategic plan.**
- **⚠️ Descriptions are about the JOB, specifications about the PERSON — and the description must come first.**
- **⚠️ EMPLOYMENT LAW HAS THRESHOLDS AT 15, 20 AND 50 EMPLOYEES ⇒ compliance is a STEP FUNCTION, not a smooth cost.**
- **⇒ the marginal cost of the 50th employee is their salary plus an entire regime.**
- **Recruitment draws on internal and external sources; contingent workers convert a fixed cost into a variable one.**
- **Six selection steps: application → interviews → tests → background → physical → probation.**
- **⚠️ RELEVANCE TO THE JOB IS THE ONLY DEFENCE FOR ANY SELECTION CRITERION** — intent is irrelevant to evidence.
- **⚠️ REPLACEMENT COSTS 75% OF SALARY (ENTRY-LEVEL) AND OVER 200% (TOP MANAGER).**
- **⚠️ THIS IS THE THIRD INDEPENDENT CONFIRMATION of [[Principles of Marketing/contents/08 - Products, Services and Brands|Marketing ch. 08]]'s \$2,500 break-even — cleared 15× and 40×.**
- **⚠️ But ch. 04's floor (6%) and ch. 07's (75%) are 12.5× apart** — different studies, recorded not filed.
- **Training: orientation, on-the-job, apprenticeship, off-the-job, online, vestibule, job simulation.**
- **⚠️ Vestibule uses SIMILAR equipment; simulation duplicates EXACT conditions** — the gap is the cost of exactness.
- **Management development adds coaching, understudy, job rotation — plus networking and mentors.**
- **⚠️ PERFORMANCE APPRAISAL IS [[06 - Management and Leadership|CH. 06]]'S CONTROL PROCESS WITH A PERSON AS THE OBJECT** — step for step, and step 1 almost verbatim.
- **⚠️ It adds one requirement control lacks: both manager and subordinate must ACCEPT the standards.**
- **Pay systems: salary, hourly, piecework, commission, bonus, profit-sharing, gain-sharing, stock options.**
- **⚠️ THEY DIFFER IN WHAT THEY MAKE MEASURABLE** — and each creates the distortion of whatever it cannot count.
- **Stock options: 10,000 shares at a \$10 strike now worth \$100 = a 10× gain, \$900,000.**
- **⚠️ FRINGE BENEFITS WENT FROM UNDER 2% OF PAYROLL TO OVER 30% — a 15× rise in share.**
- **⇒ a salary figure is about three-quarters of the true cost, so replacement costs quoted on salary understate it.**
- **Scheduling: flextime with core time, compressed workweek, home-based work, job sharing.**
- **⚠️ 24% WORK FROM HOME WEEKLY AND 68% "EXPECT TO" — deflated by Marketing ch. 09's 2.41×, that is ~28.2%.**
- **⚠️ 66% of Millennials LEFT jobs lacking flexibility — a revealed preference, and better evidence than any expectation.**

## ⚠️ Important Notes

1. **⚠️ A firm whose scarce asset is knowledge has its balance sheet leave every evening.**
2. **⚠️ Write the job description before the specification**, or you will describe a person instead of a job.
3. **⚠️ Check employee-count thresholds before hiring past 14, 19 or 49.**
4. **A rule with a cut-off creates a discontinuity, and behaviour bunches below it.**
5. **A law can create a second problem while solving the first** — affirmative action and reverse discrimination.
6. **⚠️ Interviewer intent is irrelevant to a question's evidentiary value.**
7. **Relevance to the job defends every selection instrument — tests included.**
8. **Contingent work shifts variance from the firm to the worker.**
9. **⚠️ Turnover costs 75% of salary at entry level and 200%+ at the top.**
10. **⚠️ Quote replacement cost on TOTAL COMPENSATION, not salary** — benefits add 30%+.
11. **⚠️ A retention perk costed at marginal cost almost always pays.** Now confirmed three times.
12. **Job simulation is worth its cost only where a real-job error is catastrophic.**
13. **⚠️ APPRAISAL IS CONTROL APPLIED TO A PERSON.** Everything about variance analysis applies.
14. **⚠️ Do not attribute a shortfall to its most visible cause** — 48.1%, not 100%.
15. **⚠️ Appraisal standards must be AGREED, not merely measurable.**
16. **A single appraiser is a single point of failure** — hence 360-degree review.
17. **⚠️ Choose which number the pay attaches to; that choice IS the incentive.**
18. **Every pay system distorts what it cannot count.**
19. **⚠️ Deflate every stated intention.** 68% becomes 28.2%.
20. **⚠️ Prefer a reported behaviour to a stated expectation.** "I left a job" beats "I expect to."
