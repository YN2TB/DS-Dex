# Management and Leadership

> [!note] ⚠️ New to the vault — and the least numeric chapter in it
> **No other subject covers management functions or leadership.** **But this chapter is genuinely light on arithmetic**, so the value here is **structural**: reading each of its taxonomies for what it actually orders, and connecting three of them to results the vault already owns.
>
> *(Vault note: this is **Nickels chapter 7**, and the PDF-to-book offset changes here from +2 to **−27**. Locate it by searching `_ch07_`, never by counting pages.)*

**§7 — ⚠️ HIS CONTROL PROCESS IS [[Principles of Marketing/contents/10 - Pricing|MARKETING CH. 10]]'S VARIANCE ANALYSIS, AND THE VAULT HAS THE SHARP VERSION.** **His step 3 is "compare results against standards" — and comparing a TOTAL is not the same as DECOMPOSING it.** *(Marketing ch. 10's variance bridge closed exactly — 6.5 + 5.0 + 2.0 = 13.5 — and showed the most visible cause explained only **48.1%** of a profit miss.)*

**§3 — ⚠️ THE FOURTH KIND OF PLANNING DOES NOT BELONG ON THE SPECTRUM.** **Strategic, tactical and operational planning form a hierarchy in two variables at once — time horizon AND authority level.** **Contingency planning is not a level of the organization at all; it is a plan for a STATE OF THE WORLD.** *The "cell that doesn't belong" test, which found "more for less" in [[Principles of Marketing/contents/07 - Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning|Marketing ch. 07]].*

**§5 — ⚠️ THE SKILL MIX INVERTS AS YOU RISE, WHICH MAKES PROMOTION A TRAP.** **People are promoted for TECHNICAL skill and then need CONCEPTUAL skill** — and Nickels's own figure says first-line managers "spend most of their time on technical and human relations."

**§6 — ⚠️ HE PRINTS SEVEN LEADERSHIP POSITIONS AND LABELS ONLY THREE.** **Tannenbaum & Schmidt's continuum has two axes that are COMPLEMENTS — manager's authority and employee's freedom sum to a constant — so the three "styles" are three REGIONS of one continuous variable, not three types.**

## 📘 Main Knowledge

### 1. What managers do — and how the job changed

> **Management** — the process used to accomplish organizational goals through **planning, organizing, leading and controlling** people and other organizational resources.

> [!warning] ⚠️ Nickels's opening claim is that the job description inverted
> **"The trend is to give employees as much freedom as possible."** **Managers once *told* people what to do and watched them; now they must "guide, train, support, motivate and coach."**
>
> **⇒ so the four functions survived and the METHOD changed** — which is why *leading* and *controlling* look so different in this chapter from their traditional definitions. **Controlling is no longer supervision; it is measurement against standards** (§7).

**The four functions** *(Figure 7.1)*:

| | |
|---|---|
| **planning** | anticipating trends and determining the **best strategies and tactics** to achieve goals |
| **organizing** | designing the structure and **creating conditions and systems** in which everyone works toward the goals |
| **leading** | **creating a vision** and communicating, guiding, training, coaching and motivating |
| **controlling** | **measuring performance relative to planned objectives and standards**, rewarding good work, and taking corrective action |

### 2. Planning — vision, mission, goals, objectives

| | |
|---|---|
| **vision** | a **broad explanation of why the organization exists and where it is trying to go** |
| **mission statement** | outlines the organization's **fundamental purposes** — its self-concept, philosophy, long-term survival, customer needs, social responsibility, and the nature of the product |
| **goals** | the **broad, long-term** accomplishments an organization wishes to attain |
| **objectives** | **specific, short-term** statements detailing how to achieve the goals |

> [!note] ⚠️ The vision comes BEFORE the mission, and Nickels is explicit about the order
> **"Top management usually sets the vision for the organization and then often works with others in the firm to establish a mission statement."** **⇒ the vision is authored; the mission is negotiated.** That ordering explains why mission statements read like committee documents — because they are.

**SWOT analysis** — the organization's **Strengths and Weaknesses** (internal) and the **Opportunities and Threats** it faces (external), *"usually in that order."*

> [!warning] ⚠️ The two halves of SWOT differ in a way the acronym hides
> **Strengths and weaknesses are INTERNAL and controllable; opportunities and threats are EXTERNAL and are not.** **⇒ so SWOT is really two analyses stapled together, and the useful output is the INTERACTION** — which strength addresses which opportunity, which weakness is exposed by which threat.
>
> **A SWOT that produces four lists and no pairings has done half the work.**

### 3. ⚠️ Four kinds of planning — and one that is not a kind

| | who | horizon | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| **strategic** | **top management** | **long-range** | the **major goals** and the policies, procedures, strategies and resources needed |
| **tactical** | **lower-level managers** | **short-range** | **specific short-range objectives** |
| **operational** | | | **work standards and schedules** to implement tactical objectives |
| **contingency** | | | **backup plans** in case primary plans fail |

> [!warning] ⚠️ THE FIRST THREE FORM ONE SPECTRUM. THE FOURTH IS ORTHOGONAL TO IT.
> **Strategic → tactical → operational descends in BOTH authority and time horizon simultaneously** — top/long, lower/short, work-standards/immediate. **Two variables moving together, which by now is a familiar shape:** [[03 - Doing Business in Global Markets|ch. 03]]'s market-entry continuum, [[Principles of Marketing/contents/07 - Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning|Marketing ch. 07]]'s targeting spectrum, [[Principles of Marketing/contents/11 - Marketing Channels, Retailing and Wholesaling|Marketing ch. 11]]'s distribution intensity.
>
> **⚠️ But contingency planning is not a level of the organization at all.** **It is a plan for a STATE OF THE WORLD — "if the primary plan fails" — and every level has one.**
>
> **⇒ so listing it fourth in the same figure implies a rank it does not have.** *This is the "read a taxonomy for the cell that doesn't belong" test that found "more for less" in [[Principles of Marketing/contents/07 - Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning|Marketing ch. 07]]'s value-proposition grid — and the diagnosis is the same: the odd item concedes nothing on the dimension the others trade on.*
>
> **The practical consequence: a firm can have all three levels of planning and no contingency plan, and nothing in the hierarchy will reveal the gap.**

### 4. Decision making — the six Ds

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Define** the situation |
| **2** | **Describe** and collect needed information |
| **3** | **Develop** alternatives |
| **4** | **Decide** which alternative is best |
| **5** | **Do** what is indicated — begin implementation |
| **6** | **Determine** whether the decision was a good one, and follow up |

> **Decision making** — choosing among two or more alternatives; **"the heart of all the management functions."**
> **Problem solving** — **less formal**, and usually calls for **quicker action** to resolve everyday issues.

> [!warning] ⚠️ Step 6 is the one that gets skipped, and it is the only one that produces learning
> **"Determine whether the decision was a good one, and follow up."** **⇒ without it the process is a decision *procedure*, not a decision *system*** — nothing accumulates.
>
> **And Nickels is honest that the model is often abandoned: "sometimes they have to make decisions on the spot — with little information available."** **⇒ so the six Ds describe the ideal, and the real skill is knowing which steps to compress when.** *That is [[Principles of Marketing/contents/09 - New Products and the Product Life Cycle|Marketing ch. 09]]'s test-marketing trade-off in another setting: information costs time, and time has a price.*

**PMI** — list all the **P**luses for a solution in one column, all the **M**inuses in another, and the **I**mplications in a third. *"The idea is to make sure the pluses exceed the minuses."*

> [!note] ⚠️ PMI's third column is what distinguishes it from a pros-and-cons list
> **Implications are the second-order effects — what this decision makes possible or forecloses.** **⇒ a pros-and-cons list scores the decision; PMI scores the decision AND the state it leaves you in.**

### 5. ⚠️ Organizing — three levels, three skills, and an inversion

*(Figure 7.4:)*

| level | who | responsible for |
|---|---|---|
| **top management** | president, vice presidents, CEO, CFO, COO | **overall organizational goals** and the vision |
| **middle management** | general, division, branch and plant managers *(deans and department heads)* | **tactical planning and controlling** |
| **supervisory (first-line) management** | supervisors, foremen | **directly supervising workers and evaluating daily performance** |

> [!note] Nickels notes the middle has been hollowed out
> **"Firms have eliminated many middle-level managers because fewer are needed to oversee self-managed teams and higher-skilled employees."** **⇒ the level whose job was to relay information is the level that information technology replaced.**

**Three managerial skills** *(Figure 7.5)*:

| | |
|---|---|
| **technical** | perform tasks in a **specific discipline or department** |
| **human relations** | **communication and motivation** — coaching, morale building, delegating, training, supportiveness |
| **conceptual** | **picture the organization as a whole** and see the relationships among its parts |

> [!warning] ⚠️ ALL THREE ARE NEEDED AT EVERY LEVEL, BUT THE MIX INVERTS — AND THAT MAKES PROMOTION A TRAP
> **Nickels: first-line managers "need to be skilled in all three areas. However, they spend most of their time on technical and human relations."** **⇒ conceptual skill rises with level and technical skill falls.**
>
> **⇒ SO PEOPLE ARE PROMOTED FOR THE SKILL THAT MATTERS LEAST IN THE JOB THEY ARE PROMOTED INTO.** **The best engineer becomes a manager and needs conceptual and human-relations skills that nothing in the previous job developed or tested.**
>
> **Human relations is the only one required in roughly equal measure at every level** — which is why it is the skill worth building first, and the one least often taught.

**Staffing** — getting and keeping the right people. *"To get the right kind of people to staff an organization, the firm has to offer the right kind of incentives."* **⇒ [[07 - Human Resource Management|Ch. 07]] is this function in full.**

### 6. ⚠️ Leading — a continuum, not three styles

> **Leaders vs managers:** *a manager plans, organizes and controls; a **leader** creates a vision and influences others to want to achieve it.* **The two roles overlap and are not the same job.**

**Nickels's three named styles:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **autocratic** | making managerial decisions **without consulting others** — effective **in emergencies** *(firefighting)* and with **new, unskilled workers** who need clear direction |
| **participative / democratic** | managers and employees **work together** to make decisions |
| **free-rein** | managers **set objectives** and employees are **free to do whatever is appropriate** to accomplish them |

**⚠️ But Figure 7.6 — Tannenbaum & Schmidt's classic continuum (*HBR*, 1973) — prints SEVEN positions:**

| | | style region |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manager **makes decision and announces it** | **autocratic** |
| 2 | Manager **"sells"** decision | autocratic |
| 3 | Manager **presents ideas and invites questions** | participative |
| 4 | Manager presents **tentative decision subject to change** | participative |
| 5 | Manager **presents problem, gets suggestions, makes decision** | participative |
| 6 | Manager **defines limits, asks the group to decide** | participative |
| 7 | Manager **permits employees to function within limits defined by superior** | **free rein** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ THE FIGURE'S TWO AXES ARE COMPLEMENTS — "use of authority by manager" and "area of freedom for employee" SUM TO A CONSTANT
> **⇒ so the three named styles are three REGIONS of one continuous variable, not three discrete types.** **A manager does not "have a style"; a manager occupies a position, and the position should move with the situation.**
>
> **Nickels supplies the evidence for that himself:** **autocratic leadership is right "when fighting fires" and with new unskilled workers** — *and he cites Phil Jackson using it to take a team of highly skilled individuals to three consecutive championships.* **⇒ the correct position depends on urgency and on subordinate capability, not on the leader's temperament.**
>
> **⚠️ And free-rein leadership is the most difficult, not the easiest** — it requires that objectives be stated well enough to be pursued unsupervised, which is a far harder writing task than issuing instructions.

**Three ideas that make participation work:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **empowerment** | giving frontline workers the **authority and responsibility** to respond quickly to customer requests |
| **enabling** | **giving workers the education and tools** they need to make decisions — *"the key to the success of empowerment"* |
| **transparency** | the presentation of a company's **facts and figures** in a way that is **clear and apparent to stakeholders** |

> [!warning] ⚠️ Empowerment without enabling is abandonment, and Nickels says so
> **"Without the right education, information and tools, workers cannot make good decisions."** **⇒ empowerment transfers the RIGHT to decide; enabling transfers the CAPACITY to decide well.** **Transferring the first without the second moves blame downward and nothing else.**
>
> *(His own counter-example is honest: **fast-food employees "often don't have the skill and experience to make empowerment work very well,"** so their managers supervise closely. **The correct leadership position is a function of capability — §6's continuum again.**)*

**Knowledge management** — finding the right information, **keeping it in a readily accessible place**, and **making it known to everyone in the firm.** *(He notes the data volume has grown enough that "big data" is now the term.)*

### 7. ⚠️ Controlling — the five steps, and where the value actually is

*(Figure 7.7:)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Establish clear performance standards** — *this ties planning to control* |
| **2** | **Monitor and record** actual performance |
| **3** | **Compare results against plans and standards** |
| **4** | **Communicate results** and deviations to the appropriate employees |
| **5** | **Take corrective action** when needed, and give positive feedback for good work |

**With a feedback loop asking: *are the standards realistic?***

> [!warning] ⚠️ Step 1 is load-bearing, and Nickels states the requirement exactly
> **Standards must be "specific, attainable, and measurable."** **"Without clear standards, control is impossible."** **⇒ so a vague goal is not a weak control, it is NO control** — there is nothing to compare against.
>
> **And the feedback arrow matters: "are standards realistic?"** **⇒ a persistent variance is evidence about the standard as well as about performance**, and a control system that never questions its own targets will keep reporting failure at a target nobody could hit.

> [!warning] ⚠️ STEP 3 IS THE WHOLE VALUE, AND COMPARING A TOTAL IS NOT DECOMPOSING IT
> **[[Principles of Marketing/contents/10 - Pricing|Marketing ch. 10]] ran exactly this process on a real profit-and-loss statement, and the result is the sharp version of Nickels's step 3:**
>
> | cause | \$ impact | share of the miss |
> |---|---|---|
> | sales \$25m short, at the planned margin | 6,500,000 | **48.15%** |
> | **margin erosion** 26% → 21% | 5,000,000 | **37.04%** |
> | **fixed cost overrun** | 2,000,000 | 14.81% |
> | **TOTAL** | **13,500,000** | **100%** |
>
> **The bridge closed exactly — and "we missed our sales forecast," the most visible cause, explained only 48.1%.**
>
> **⇒ SO STEP 3 DONE AS A SINGLE COMPARISON PRODUCES ONE NUMBER AND ONE SCAPEGOAT.** **Done as a decomposition it produces three causes with three owners** — which is what step 4 ("communicate results to the appropriate employees") actually requires. **Nickels's five steps are correct and under-specified at exactly the point where the work is.**

**A new criterion for measurement — customer satisfaction.** *"Traditional measures of success are usually financial… Certainly these measures are still important, but they're not the whole purpose of the firm."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **external customers** | **dealers** who buy to resell, and **ultimate customers (end users)** who buy for personal use |
| **internal customers** | **individuals and units within the firm that receive services from other individuals or units** |

> [!note] ⚠️ "Internal customers" is [[Principles of Marketing/contents/08 - Products, Services and Brands|Marketing ch. 08]]'s internal marketing, and the vault has its mechanism
> **Marketing ch. 08's service-profit chain: internal service quality → satisfied employees → greater service value → satisfied customers → profit** — and its rule that **"internal marketing must precede external marketing."**
>
> **⇒ so the internal-customer idea is not a metaphor; it is a claim that the chain has a direction.** *(And Marketing ch. 08 costed one link of it: the Four Seasons' retention perk breaks even at a \$2,500 replacement cost — which [[04 - Ethics and Corporate Social Responsibility|ch. 04]]'s "6–213% of salary" independently confirmed.)*

**Zappos, costed:** team leaders are required to spend **at least 20% of their time off the job with their team members.**

| | |
|---|---|
| 20% of a 40-hour week | **8 hours — one full day a week** |
| over a 48-week year | **384 hours = 9.6 working weeks** |
| a leader with 10 reports | **48 minutes per person per week** |

> [!note] ⚠️ That is a large, real, and deliberately charged cost
> **It falls on the LEADER's time, not the team's.** **For scale, [[Principles of Marketing/contents/12 - Integrated Marketing Communications and Digital Marketing|Marketing ch. 12]] found that a sales rep's actual selling time is only 37% of working time — and that sales-force size scales as $1/TA$.** **⇒ carving out 20% of a manager's week is the same kind of decision, and Zappos made it explicit rather than letting it happen by default.**

## 📝 Summary

- **Management = planning, organizing, leading, controlling.** **⚠️ The functions survived; the METHOD inverted toward coaching and freedom.**
- **Vision → mission → goals → objectives.** ⚠️ **The vision is authored; the mission is negotiated.**
- **⚠️ SWOT is two analyses stapled together** — internal/controllable vs external/not. **Four lists and no pairings is half the work.**
- **Four kinds of planning: strategic (top, long), tactical (lower, short), operational (standards and schedules), contingency (backup).**
- **⚠️ THE FIRST THREE ARE ONE SPECTRUM IN TWO VARIABLES; CONTINGENCY IS ORTHOGONAL** — a plan for a state of the world, which every level needs.
- **⇒ a firm can have all three levels and no contingency plan, and the hierarchy will not reveal it.**
- **The six Ds: Define, Describe, Develop, Decide, Do, Determine.**
- **⚠️ Step 6 is skipped and is the only one that produces learning.**
- **PMI adds a third column — implications — so it scores the state the decision leaves you in.**
- **Three levels: top, middle, supervisory.** **The middle was hollowed out by information technology.**
- **Three skills: technical, human relations, conceptual.**
- **⚠️ THE MIX INVERTS WITH LEVEL ⇒ PEOPLE ARE PROMOTED FOR THE SKILL THAT MATTERS LEAST IN THE NEW JOB.**
- **⚠️ Human relations is the only skill needed equally at every level** — build it first.
- **Leaders create a vision; managers plan, organize and control.** The roles overlap.
- **⚠️ FIGURE 7.6 SHOWS SEVEN POSITIONS ON ONE CONTINUUM, NOT THREE STYLES** — authority and freedom are complements summing to a constant.
- **⚠️ The right position depends on URGENCY and SUBORDINATE CAPABILITY, not on temperament.** Autocratic is right for firefighting.
- **⚠️ Free-rein is the hardest style** — it requires objectives good enough to pursue unsupervised.
- **⚠️ EMPOWERMENT WITHOUT ENABLING IS ABANDONMENT** — one transfers the right to decide, the other the capacity.
- **Transparency = facts and figures clear to stakeholders. Knowledge management = find it, store it accessibly, make it known.**
- **Controlling: establish standards → monitor → compare → communicate → correct**, with feedback on whether standards are realistic.
- **⚠️ A vague goal is NO control, not a weak one.**
- **⚠️ STEP 3 AS A SINGLE COMPARISON GIVES ONE SCAPEGOAT; AS A DECOMPOSITION IT GIVES THREE CAUSES WITH THREE OWNERS** — [[Principles of Marketing/contents/10 - Pricing|Marketing ch. 10]]'s variance bridge, where the most visible cause was 48.1%.
- **External vs internal customers** — the second is [[Principles of Marketing/contents/08 - Products, Services and Brands|Marketing ch. 08]]'s service-profit chain, and it claims a direction.
- **Zappos requires leaders to spend 20% of time with their teams = one day a week = 9.6 working weeks a year.**
