# Marketing — Helping Buyers Buy

> [!warning] ⚠️ TOTAL OVERLAP — this chapter is owned twelve times over
> **[[Principles of Marketing/contents/00-Index|Principles of Marketing]] covers every topic here across twelve notes, and covers each of them far more deeply.** **⇒ this note is deliberately SHORT.** It gives the business-management framing, points at the owner for each topic, and spends its length on the one thing Nickels does better than Kotler.
>
> *(Vault note: Nickels chapter 13; PDF-to-book offset **−130**. Locate by searching `_ch13_`.)*

**§1 — ⚠️ NICKELS HAS THE SHARPER DEFINITION OF MARKETING, AND ANOTHER SUBJECT PROVES IT.** **He says marketing is *"about helping the buyer buy"*; Kotler says it is *"engaging customers and managing profitable customer relationships."*** **The difference is a claim about WHO IS ACTIVE — and [[Principles of Marketing/contents/12 - Integrated Marketing Communications and Digital Marketing|Marketing ch. 12]] measured it: buyers complete 57–60% of the buying process before contacting a seller, and $4/7 = 57.14\%$, so the buyer self-serves FOUR of the seven selling steps.** ***His slogan is not a slogan; it is vindicated by a number in another book.***

**§5 — ⚠️ "ONLY 5 PERCENT OF AMERICANS" IS 16.25 MILLION PEOPLE.** **"Niche" is a share, not a size** — and [[Principles of Marketing/contents/07 - Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning|Marketing ch. 07]]'s *substantial* criterion asks whether a segment is large enough to serve, which 16 million plainly is.

## 📘 Main Knowledge

### 1. ⚠️ What marketing is — and the better definition

> **Marketing** — the activity, set of institutions and processes for **creating, communicating, delivering and exchanging offerings** that have value for customers, clients, partners and society at large.

**But Nickels's working definition is the one worth keeping:** *"Marketing is instead about **helping the buyer buy**."*

> [!warning] ⚠️ That is a claim about who is active, and it is now measurable
> **Kotler's definition — "engaging customers and managing profitable customer relationships" — puts the FIRM in the active role.** **Nickels puts the BUYER there and casts the firm as an assistant.**
>
> **⇒ and [[Principles of Marketing/contents/12 - Integrated Marketing Communications and Digital Marketing|Marketing ch. 12]] settles which is right:**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | business buyers who **start their search online** | **92%** |
> | of the buying process completed **before contacting a seller** | **"nearly 60%"** |
> | and $4/7$ of the seven-step selling process | **57.14%** |
>
> **⇒ THE BUYER SELF-SERVES FOUR OF THE SEVEN SELLING STEPS** — prospecting, preapproach, approach and presentation. **The seller now arrives at "handling objections."**
>
> **So Nickels, writing a survey text, states the position that the specialist text's own figures support and its definition does not.** *Worth remembering: a simpler book is not always the less accurate one.*

**The four eras of marketing:** **production → selling → marketing concept → customer relationship** *(and now, he adds, an emerging **mobile/on-demand** era)*.

**The marketing concept has three parts:** a **customer orientation**, a **service orientation** (everyone in the organization has the same objective), and a **profit orientation**.

### 2. The four Ps

| | |
|---|---|
| **Product** | any physical good, service or idea that satisfies a want — including **brand name**, packaging and **test marketing** |
| **Price** | what to charge, given competition, production costs and consumer expectations |
| **Place** | getting it to the buyer — intermediaries, wholesalers, retailers |
| **Promotion** | advertising, personal selling, public relations, publicity, sales promotion, word of mouth |

> [!note] ⚠️ Depth lives elsewhere for all four, and the vault's results are much sharper
> | | owner | the result there |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Product** | [[Principles of Marketing/contents/08 - Products, Services and Brands|Marketing ch. 08]] | three levels; a package gets **0.20 seconds**; a 98% standard is an error count compounding to **83.37%** over nine stages |
> | **Price** | [[Principles of Marketing/contents/10 - Pricing|Marketing ch. 10]] | required elasticity of a price cut is $1/(m-d)$; **profit change from a price rise is $d/m$** |
> | **Place** | [[Principles of Marketing/contents/11 - Marketing Channels, Retailing and Wholesaling|Marketing ch. 11]] | an intermediary's saving is $(M-1)(C-1)-1$ — **zero at 2×2, 98.90% at 100×1,000** |
> | **Promotion** | [[Principles of Marketing/contents/12 - Integrated Marketing Communications and Digital Marketing|Marketing ch. 12]] | **CPM cannot rank media** — a Super Bowl spot is \$45.05/thousand against banner rates of \$25–\$50 |
>
> **⇒ do not re-derive any of this here.**

### 3. The marketing research process — four steps

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Define the question** and determine the present situation |
| **2** | **Collect research data** — secondary first *(already published)*, then primary *(new)* |
| **3** | **Analyze the research data** |
| **4** | **Choose the best solution and implement it** |

> [!note] ⚠️ Step 2's ordering is the practical rule
> **Secondary data before primary, always** — because secondary data is cheaper and often sufficient, and because it tells you what primary research still needs to answer. *(A researcher who collects primary data first pays for facts already in print.)*
>
> **The depth is [[Principles of Marketing/contents/04 - Managing Marketing Information and Research|Marketing ch. 04]]'s**, which adds what this chapter cannot: **probability versus non-probability sampling as a hard boundary**, and the multiplicity problem — **testing 20 segments gives a 64.2% chance of at least one false positive.**

### 4. Environmental scanning — four influences

**Global · technological · sociocultural · competitive · economic factors.**

> [!note] Owned by [[Principles of Marketing/contents/03 - Analyzing the Marketing Environment|Marketing ch. 03]]
> **Which computed the results this list only gestures at: boomers earn 2.69× and spend 1.92× but convert at 0.43×; Gen Z's influence ratio is 13.6×.**

### 5. ⚠️ Segmentation — and what "niche" actually means

| | |
|---|---|
| **geographic** | by city, county, state, region |
| **demographic** | age, income, education, gender, occupation |
| **psychographic** | values, attitudes, interests |
| **benefit** | the **benefit sought** — *"determining who are the biggest eaters of healthy food"* |
| **volume (usage)** | by **how much** they buy |

**Then four targeting approaches:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **mass marketing** | products and promotions to please **large groups**, with little segmentation |
| **niche marketing** | identifying **small but profitable** segments and designing products for them |
| **one-to-one marketing** | a **unique mix** for each individual customer |
| **relationship marketing** | moving away from mass production toward **custom-made goods for a smaller, loyal group** |

**⚠️ And his own example makes "niche" quantifiable:** *"with only 5 percent of Americans identifying themselves as [vegetarian], [the market] supports more specialized niche products."*

> [!warning] ⚠️ 5% OF 325 MILLION AMERICANS IS 16,250,000 PEOPLE
> **That is larger than the population of most countries.**
>
> **⇒ "NICHE" IS A SHARE, NOT A SIZE.** **[[Principles of Marketing/contents/07 - Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning|Marketing ch. 07]]'s *substantial* criterion asks whether a segment is "large or profitable enough to serve"** — and 16 million people plainly is.
>
> **The word "only" is doing rhetorical work that the arithmetic contradicts**, and this is the same habit that runs through the whole vault: **a share is not a quantity until you multiply it by the base.**
>
> *(And Marketing ch. 07 supplies the decision rule this chapter lacks: at a 17.5% price premium a niche player **can lose 30.4% of volume and break even** — which is how you decide whether a 16-million-person segment is worth the narrower reach.)*

### 6. Consumer behaviour and the B2B market

**The consumer decision-making process:** **problem recognition → information search → alternative evaluation → purchase decision → post-purchase evaluation (cognitive dissonance).**

**Influences:** learning · reference group · **culture** · **subculture** · cognitive dissonance.

**⚠️ The business-to-business market differs, and Nickels's own contrast is the size asymmetry:** *"a relatively small number of construction firms or mining operations compared to the over 125 million [consumers]."*

> [!warning] ⚠️ That asymmetry is exactly [[Principles of Marketing/contents/06 - Business Markets and Business Buyer Behavior|Marketing ch. 06]]'s "fewer, larger buyers" — and the vault priced it
> **Two results this chapter states qualitatively and the owner computes:**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **derived demand elasticity** ≈ cost share × final elasticity | **0.30**, from a final-goods elasticity of 1.5 |
> | **the accelerator** | a **+10%** change in final demand **DOUBLES** equipment orders; **−10% ELIMINATES them**; the amplification equals the **asset life** |
>
> **⇒ so the size asymmetry is not a curiosity. It is why B2B demand is INELASTIC in its own price and VIOLENTLY ELASTIC in the final market** — which is the single most useful fact about industrial selling, and it cannot be reached from this chapter alone.

**His other B2B differences:** buyers are **geographically concentrated**, buy in **larger quantities**, are **more rational and less emotional**, and the selling is more **personal and relationship-based**.

*(His Michelle Phan opener, for scale: **more than 8 million YouTube subscribers** and a beauty start-up **valued at more than \$500 million** — **\$62.50 per subscriber**. Read as [[Principles of Marketing/contents/08 - Products, Services and Brands|Marketing ch. 08]] reads a brand value, $V = X/(k-g)$, that stock implies **\$40 million a year** of attributable profit at $k-g=8\%$ — **\$5.00 per subscriber per year.** **Stating a valuation as a FLOW makes it checkable; as a stock it is not.**)*

## ⚠️ Important Notes

1. **⚠️ A simpler book can hold the sharper definition.** Test definitions against data, not authority.
2. **⚠️ The buyer now does four of the seven selling steps.** Prospecting means being findable.
3. **Secondary data before primary, always.**
4. **⚠️ A share is not a quantity until multiplied by the base.** 5% is 16 million.
5. **⚠️ "Niche" describes a share; the decision needs a break-even.**

9. **Capital-goods cyclicality is the accelerator, and its amplification is the asset life.**
10. **⚠️ State a valuation as a FLOW to make it checkable.** \$500m at $k-g=8\%$ is \$40m a year.
