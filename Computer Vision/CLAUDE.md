## Findings worth keeping
- **ch. 14 — ⭐ THE SUBJECT'S CLOSING RESULT: EVERY 3D METHOD IS AN ANSWER TO ONE LINE FROM WEEK 1, AND EXACTLY ONE OF THEM *MEASURES* DEPTH.** CV ch. 01 §3 stated $x=fX/Z$ maps every point on a ray to the same pixel, so $Z$ is destroyed. Fourteen weeks later: **stereo** (two views + baseline → triangulate), **SfM** (many views, poses unknown → solve jointly), **MVS** (poses known → dense depth), **monocular** (*a PRIOR over object sizes, not a measurement*), **LiDAR/ToF** (*measures $Z$ directly, bypassing vision*), **NeRF/splatting** (optimise a field to explain all views). ⇒ ***only LiDAR MEASURES; every other one INFERS*** — **and their failure modes do NOT overlap** (stereo on texture and range, SfM on scale, monocular on unusual objects, LiDAR on reflective/transparent surfaces). **So the first question about any reported depth is WHICH ROW PRODUCED IT — a depth map does not record its provenance.** *(And the subject's arc in three lines: hand-designed FEATURES → learned features (ch. 02→04); hand-designed PIPELINE STAGES → learned end-to-end (ch. 07→08); task-specific TRAINING → task-specific PROMPTING (ch. 12→14). **Each step deletes a human-specified component**, leaving architecture, objective and data — and ch. 12 showed the data is now UNAUDITED, which is where the open problems moved.)*
- **ch. 14 — STEREO DEPTH ERROR IS QUADRATIC IN DEPTH, AND THAT ONE EXPONENT DECIDES THE CAMERA-VS-LIDAR ARGUMENT.** $Z=fB/d \Rightarrow |\Delta Z| = \frac{Z^2}{fB}|\Delta d|$. A rig at $f{=}800$ px, $B{=}0.20$ m, $\pm0.25$ px disparity: **0.16% at 1 m, 1.56% at 10 m, 15.62% — ±15.6 METRES — at 100 m.** *Five times the distance is TWENTY-FIVE times the error, and the RELATIVE error grows linearly, so there is no range at which stereo is merely "somewhat worse" — it degrades without limit.* ⚠️ **AND THE OBVIOUS FIX IS SELF-LIMITING: a wider baseline helps linearly (2.50 m → 0.125 m error at 20 m from $B{=}0.05$ to 1.00 m) but means FEWER SHARED PIXELS, MORE OCCLUSION and a HARDER correspondence problem — trading a computable error for an uncomputable one.** ⇒ ***long-range perception uses LiDAR/radar because a time-of-flight error is roughly CONSTANT with range while stereo's is quadratic.*** **Reporting rule: a stereo rig's accuracy is a CURVE, not a number — always quote it with its range.** *(Sub-pixel disparity matters quadratically too: 1 px → 0.25 px is a 4× depth improvement at EVERY range, usually cheaper than a longer baseline and with no occlusion cost.)*
- **ch. 14 — MONOCULAR SCALE AMBIGUITY IS A PROOF, NOT A DIFFICULTY.** $f(sX)/(sZ)=fX/Z$ **identically for every $s$** — verified at $s = 0.5, 1, 2, 100$, all giving 160.0000 px ⇒ ***a toy car at 50 cm and a real car at 10 m produce the IDENTICAL image.*** **So a "monocular depth estimation" network reports RELATIVE depth, or absolute depth only from LEARNED OBJECT SIZES — a PRIOR, not a measurement — and it fails SILENTLY on scale models, unusual objects, photographs of photographs and unfamiliar domains.** *That distinction is a safety property, not a technicality: if you need metric depth you need a second view, a known baseline, a known object size, or a range sensor — no architecture supplies it.* **Same class of statement as CV ch. 03 §5's proof about linear models: a property of the formulation that no data fixes.** ⚠️ **And it generalises: SfM recovers a scene only up to a 7-DoF SIMILARITY (3 translation + 3 rotation + 1 SCALE), so anyone quoting metres from a pure SfM pipeline has assumed something they did not state.** *(Even calibrated two-view geometry cannot supply it — $\mathbf E$'s 5 DoF are rotation (3) + translation DIRECTION (2); the magnitude is the same missing scale.)*
- **ch. 14 — NeRF NEEDS 122,880,000 MLP EVALUATIONS PER $800^2$ FRAME, AND IT IS AN OPTIMISATION DRESSED AS LEARNING.** $800\times800\times192$ samples/ray = **122.88 million forward passes for ONE image**; 30 fps would need **3.69 BILLION per second.** ⇒ ***that number, not any quality argument, is why 3D Gaussian splatting (2023) displaced NeRF for real-time use*** — it rasterises explicit 3D Gaussians with **no network in the rendering loop at all**, trading a compact IMPLICIT function (~5 MB of weights) for an EXPLICIT primitive soup (hundreds of MB). ⚠️ **And the conceptual point that makes NeRF teachable: its TRAINING SET IS ONE SCENE and its OUTPUT IS THE WEIGHTS — it does not generalise and is not meant to. Its loss is PHOTOMETRIC REPROJECTION ERROR, which is classical multi-view stereo's objective EXACTLY** ⇒ ***the "neural" part is the REPRESENTATION, not the objective.*** **Budget NeRF and splatting as reconstruction jobs, not training runs.** *(Also here: the epipolar constraint turns a 2D search into a 1D one — 307,200 candidates → 640, a 480× reduction by EXACT geometry — and bundle adjustment's 150,600 unknowns are solvable ONLY because the Jacobian is sparse.)*
- **ch. 13 — A MODEL THAT MEMORISES ITS TRAINING SET SCORES FID = 0.000000, WHICH IS *BETTER* THAN A MODEL THAT GENERALISES (1.0689).** FID fits Gaussians to features and compares them, so **an exact copy of the training set reproduces $\mu$ and $\Sigma$ identically and scores PERFECT**, while a genuine generaliser is penalised by its own sampling noise. ⇒ ***the field's standard metric measures DISTRIBUTIONAL MATCH and is STRUCTURALLY BLIND TO NOVELTY*** — it must be reported with a nearest-neighbour memorisation check. ⚠️ **AND THIS IS NOT A BENCHMARKING NICETY: whether a generator reproduces its training data is EXACTLY the question in the copyright and privacy disputes about image generators, and the standard metric is designed not to notice.** *(FID is also blind to any individual sample's quality, biased downward with more samples, and not comparable across Inception implementations.)* **THIRD time in this subject that the headline metric hides the failure that matters** — after ch. 08's AP conventions and ch. 09's pixel accuracy. *(The FID here uses 64-d Gaussian features, so absolute values are not comparable to published ones — the ORDERING is exact and feature-space-independent, because a copy reproduces the reference statistics by construction.)*
- **ch. 13 — THE GAN'S SATURATING LOSS HAS A GRADIENT *BOUNDED BY 1* EXACTLY WHEN THE GENERATOR IS LOSING, AND WITH DISJOINT SUPPORTS THE OBJECTIVE IS A CONSTANT.** $\frac{d}{dD}\log(1-D)=\frac1{D-1}$ so $|\cdot|\to1$ as $D\to0$, while $\frac{d}{dD}(-\log D)$ diverges: at $D(G(z)){=}10^{-4}$ the two give **1.0001 vs 10,000 — a ratio of 9,999.** *$D$ small means the discriminator is WINNING — precisely when the generator most needs a signal* ⇒ **Goodfellow's own paper proposes the non-saturating form for this reason: the minimax game as WRITTEN is not the game as PLAYED.** ⚠️ **And the deeper problem (Arjovsky & Bottou 2017): two low-dimensional manifolds in $\mathbb R^{196608}$ generically have DISJOINT supports, a perfect discriminator EXISTS, and JS is then CONSTANT at $\log 2 = 0.693147$ — zero gradient everywhere.** ⇒ ***the objective reports THAT the generator is wrong and carries no information about HOW wrong; Wasserstein distance fixes exactly this by measuring how far apart they are.*** **Most extreme form yet of the vault's "a loss that switches off" pattern** (CV ch. 03 §7's hinge, ch. 10 §5's triplet): not saturating on easy examples — *constant everywhere.*
- **ch. 13 — DIFFUSION BUYS AN OBJECTIVE THAT CANNOT SATURATE OR COLLAPSE AND PAYS 1,000× AT SAMPLING TIME — AND THAT IS THE BETTER KIND OF PROBLEM.** Training is $L=\|\varepsilon-\varepsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2$: **a plain supervised L2 regression, no adversary, no minimax, a target that always exists**, made possible because the forward process has a closed form at every $t$ ($\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$ falls 0.99995 → **0.006353** at $T{=}1000$, so the image is genuinely gone and the reverse process can start from pure noise). **The cost: 1,000 network evaluations per image against a GAN's 1** — hence DDIM (50), distillation/consistency (~4). ⇒ ***a GAN puts its cost in TRAINING and makes it a STABILITY problem; diffusion puts it in SAMPLING and makes it an ARITHMETIC problem — and arithmetic yields to engineering***, which is precisely what DDIM and distillation did. ⚠️ **Corollary for reporting: quoting a diffusion model's speed without its STEP COUNT is meaningless**, and classifier-free guidance costs a further exact 2× (and $w>1$ EXTRAPOLATES past the conditional prediction, which is why large $w$ degrades images while sharpening prompt adherence).
- **ch. 13 — LATENT DIFFUSION'S 48× COMPRESSION IS 4,096× ON THE ATTENTION, AND THAT — NOT A MODELLING ADVANCE — IS WHY TEXT-TO-IMAGE RUNS ON ONE CONSUMER GPU.** $512^2\times3 = 786{,}432$ values → $64^2\times4 = 16{,}384$ (**48.0×**), and since the U-Net's attention is quadratic in tokens (CV ch. 06 §1), $262{,}144 \to 4{,}096$ tokens is $6.87\times10^{10} \to 1.68\times10^7$ = **4,096×** — *with the U-Net running 1,000 times on the small one.* ⇒ **an ENGINEERING DECOMPOSITION: a separately trained, frozen autoencoder does the PERCEPTUAL compression, the diffusion model does the SEMANTIC generation.** ⚠️ **Also here: MODE COLLAPSE IS A SPECIFICATION FAILURE — there is NO TERM in the GAN objective that mentions coverage**, so a generator emitting one perfect image is not penalised at all. **SECOND SETTING IN TWO CHAPTERS for ch. 12 §4's rule** — *a degenerate solution the objective does not forbid, fixed by changing the objective or the architecture and NEVER the schedule; monitor the degenerate quantity directly, because the loss will not show it.* *(And VAE blurriness is ch. 10 §1's mode averaging: a per-pixel Gaussian likelihood is minimised by averaging over plausible outputs — the average of two plausible images is an image of neither.)*
- **ch. 12 — InfoNCE CANNOT CERTIFY MORE THAN $\log N$ NATS, SO BATCH SIZE IS A HARD INFORMATION CEILING — AND A BATCH OF 256 IS WORTH 0.8 CLASS LABELS PER EXAMPLE.** Oord et al.'s bound $I(x;y)\ge\log N-L$ caps the certifiable mutual information at $\log N$ **whatever the model does**: $N{=}256 \to$ **5.5452 nats = exactly 8 bits**, $N{=}65{,}536 \to$ 16 bits — against **one ImageNet label's $\log_2 1000 = 9.97$ bits.** ⇒ ***that single inequality explains the architectural split: SimCLR ENLARGES THE BATCH (4,096, 128 TPU cores), MoCo QUEUES 65,536 negatives at batch 256 — 256× the batch — and therefore NEEDS a momentum encoder, because queued keys came from an older network.*** **Two designs, one bound.** ⚠️ **AND THE BEST DIAGNOSTIC IN THE METHOD: $L = \log N$ EXACTLY at initialisation** (verified to $10^{-12}$ at $N{=}256$ and 4,096) ⇒ ***a contrastive loss still sitting at $\log N$ has learned NOTHING — visible at a glance instead of after a linear probe. Print $\log N$ beside the loss.***
- **ch. 12 — COLLAPSE IS THE GLOBAL OPTIMUM OF THE NON-CONTRASTIVE OBJECTIVE, NOT A BAD LOCAL MINIMUM.** BYOL/SimSiam maximise $\cos(f(v_1),f(v_2))$; **a constant output scores cosine exactly 1.000000 — the LOWEST POSSIBLE loss, reachable from any input** (an informative pair scored 0.3046). ⇒ ***the loss ALONE cannot prevent collapse; only the ARCHITECTURE can*** — stop-gradient on the target branch (**SimSiam's essential ingredient**), a predictor head on one branch only, an EMA target encoder, or an explicit anti-collapse term (Barlow Twins, VICReg). **Contrastive methods are immune BY CONSTRUCTION: negatives make the constant solution MAXIMALLY bad, since every negative also scores 1.** ⚠️ ***GENERAL RULE WORTH CARRYING EVERYWHERE: when a DEGENERATE solution is a GLOBAL optimum of your objective, no amount of training, tuning or data fixes it — the objective or the architecture must change.*** *(Same class of reasoning as CV ch. 03 §5's proof that no linear model can score an image and its negative highly: a property of the formulation, not of the optimisation.)* **Corollary: NEVER conclude "no collapse" from a falling loss — monitor the embedding standard deviation or its covariance spectrum.**
- **ch. 12 — A PRETEXT TASK IS ONLY AS GOOD AS THE SHORTCUT IT FAILS TO ADMIT, AND THAT TEST RECURS THREE TIMES IN ONE CHAPTER.** Jigsaw is solved by **matching chromatic aberration at the tile borders**, rotation by **finding the sky**, inpainting by **copying neighbouring texture** — a network that finds the shortcut solves the task perfectly and learns nothing transferable. ⇒ ***designing a pretext task is really the ADVERSARIAL exercise of anticipating shortcuts***, which is why hand-designed ones were displaced by augmentation- and masking-defined tasks spanning a far larger space. **But it does not disappear: SimCLR needs colour jitter WITH crop because matching two crops by colour histogram is a shortcut, and MAE's 75% mask rate is set so that INTERPOLATION is not one.** *(Jigsaw's full space is $9! = 362{,}880$ classes — unusable, hence the chosen 1,000-permutation subset with large mutual Hamming distance.)*
- **ch. 12 — MAE's 75% MASK IS AN EFFICIENCY ARGUMENT FIRST, AND CLIP RECOVERS CV ch. 03's LINEAR CLASSIFIER EXACTLY.** **(i)** At 75% the encoder sees **49 of 196 tokens** ⇒ **attention costs 16.00× less and the linear terms 4.00× less** — *MAE is fast DESPITE reconstructing pixels precisely because the expensive encoder NEVER SEES the masked patches, unlike BERT, which pushes `[MASK]` through the full model; the decoder is lightweight and discarded.* **75% works in vision where 15% is right for text because images are SPATIALLY REDUNDANT** — a word is nearly irreplaceable, a patch is usually interpolable (CV ch. 11 measured the temporal version at 3.33 px between frames). **(ii)** ⚠️ **CLIP's TEXT EMBEDDINGS *ARE* THE ROWS OF $\mathbf W$** — scoring is still $\mathbf W\mathbf x$ and nothing about the classification changed; **what changed is that $\mathbf W$'s rows were learned from LANGUAGE instead of labelled images** ⇒ *a new class costs one text-encoder forward pass, not a retrained head.* **"Zero-shot" is a STRUCTURAL change, not an accuracy improvement.** *Limits: performance tracks web representation, the 400M pairs are unaudited, and prompt wording changes results — CV ch. 08's "name the protocol" in a new place.*
- **ch. 11 — THE APERTURE PROBLEM IS ONE EQUATION IN TWO UNKNOWNS, AND THE MATRIX THAT FIXES IT IS CV ch. 02's HARRIS STRUCTURE TENSOR — THE SAME $2\times2$ MATRIX, SOLVED FOR A DIFFERENT REASON.** Brightness constancy linearises to $I_xu+I_yv+I_t=0$: **one equation, two unknowns ⇒ a LINE of solutions**, so only the **normal flow** ($-I_t/\|\nabla I\|$, along the gradient) is recoverable and *a moving edge appears to move perpendicular to itself.* Built $A^\top A$ for three synthetic windows: **flat $(0,0)$; single edge $(0,\,123{,}750)$ — RANK 1, still unsolvable despite enormous gradient energy; corner $(61{,}875,\,73{,}125)$.** ⇒ ***RANK, NOT MAGNITUDE, decides solvability — check $\lambda_{\min}$, never the gradient energy*** — and ***that is WHY classical trackers track corners: corners are the only points where the flow equation has a unique solution.*** **Lucas–Kanade and Horn–Schunck both simply MANUFACTURE EQUATIONS** (constant-flow window; global smoothness), and deep flow nets do the same with a learned prior — **none of them repeals the ambiguity.** *Third instance in this subject of one operator reached by independent routes.*
- **ch. 11 — IDENTITY ERRORS COMPOUND AND DETECTION ERRORS DO NOT, AND 99% PER-FRAME ASSOCIATION IS CATASTROPHIC.** A track survives $T$ frames with probability $p^T$: at $p{=}0.99$, **73.97% for one second, 4.90% for ten, $1.4\times10^{-6}\%$ for one minute.** Holding a one-minute track with 95% probability needs $p\ge0.999972$ — an error below $2.85\times10^{-5}$ per frame, **351× stricter than 1%.** ⇒ ***a MISS is local and self-repairing (the object is redetected next frame); an ID SWITCH is PERMANENT — every later frame inherits the wrong label*** — **which is why MOTA/IDF1/HOTA count them separately rather than averaging them into one score.** ⚠️ **SECOND CHAPTER RUNNING for this arithmetic: it is ch. 10's gallery FAR in the time dimension — $1-(1-\varepsilon)^N$ over people, $p^T$ over frames.** ***General rule now worth applying everywhere: when a per-unit error rate is applied repeatedly, quote the COMPOUNDED number, never the per-unit one.***
- **ch. 11 — (2+1)D SAVES 2.25× AT $3\times3\times3$ AND THE SAVING IS THE LESSER BENEFIT.** $3\times3\times3=27$ vs $9+3=12$; **4.17× at $5\times5\times5$, 3.94× at $7\times3\times3$** — ratio $\frac{k_tk_hk_w}{k_hk_w+k_t}$, **exactly the SHAPE of CV ch. 05's depthwise-separable formula, and for the same reason: factorising a JOINT operation into two MARGINAL ones.** ⚠️ **But the real gain is that the factorisation inserts a NONLINEARITY between the spatial and temporal halves ⇒ MORE nonlinearity at a SMALLER parameter budget — VGG's $3\times3$-stacking argument, in time.** *(A 3D conv is exactly 3× a 2D one at every kernel size — verified at $k{=}3,5,7$.)* **Video transformers factorise space-time attention the same way, and ⚠️ in VIDEO the quadratic genuinely dominates** — CV ch. 06 found attention only 36.1% of the work at $224^2$, but video passes the $n>2d$ crossover easily.
- **ch. 11 — VIDEO IS A DATA-VOLUME PROBLEM WHOSE REDUNDANCY CANCELS MOST OF ITS OWN COST.** A 10-s clip at 30 fps is **172.27 MB fp32 for ONE example** (300× one image); a modest 8 clips × 16 frames is 73.5 MB of INPUT before any activation is stored ⇒ **batch size is the first casualty.** *But* at 30 fps a point moving 100 px/s moves **3.33 px between frames** ⇒ **the marginal information per frame is tiny**, which is why clip models sample 16–32 frames rather than all 300 and sparse sampling works nearly as well as dense. **The video is large; the CONTENT is not.** *(Also here: two-stream's pre-computed optical flow was a HAND-DESIGNED STAGE inside a learned pipeline — DL ch. 06 §9's deletion pattern — duly deleted by 3D convs and video transformers, but surviving where DATA IS SCARCE, because it is a prior that needs no learning.)*
- **ch. 10 — FACE IDENTIFICATION NEEDS A FALSE-ACCEPT RATE FOUR ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE BETTER THAN VERIFICATION, AND THE ARITHMETIC IS UNFORGIVING.** With per-comparison FAR, the chance of at least one false match in a gallery of $N$ is $1-(1-\mathrm{FAR})^N$: **a 0.1% FAR — which sounds excellent — gives 9.52% at $N{=}100$ and 99.995% at $N{=}10{,}000$.** To hold gallery-level error under 1%: $N{=}10^4$ needs FAR $\le 1.005\times10^{-6}$, **$N{=}10^6$ needs $1.005\times10^{-8}$ — one in 99.5 million, ~$10^5\times$ stricter than the quoted figure.** ⇒ ***quoting a VERIFICATION FAR for an IDENTIFICATION system overstates reliability by ~4 orders of magnitude, and the errors land on people who were never in the gallery.*** **Same mathematics as the multiple-comparisons problem: one test at $\alpha$ is fine, $N$ tests at $\alpha$ are not.** *Two facts belong with it and are stated qualitatively (external, unverifiable here): FAR is NOT uniform across demographic groups (NIST FRVT), and in a search for a rare individual the BASE RATE makes most positives false regardless of FAR.* **A system reported only as "99.9% accurate" has not been described.**
- **ch. 10 — A REGRESSOR MUST AVERAGE ITS MODES, AND THE AVERAGE OF TWO PLAUSIBLE ELBOWS IS ON NEITHER ARM.** That, not accuracy, is why heatmaps replaced direct coordinate regression: two equally likely positions at 0.2 and 0.8 give **0.5**. *A heatmap represents both modes; a regressor cannot represent the ambiguity at all* — **DL ch. 01's `argmax` point again: reporting a single value asserts a unimodal belief.** Costs: **2,048× more output** ($17\times64^2 = 69{,}632$ vs 34 numbers) and **a $\pm s/2$ quantisation floor — ±2 px at stride 4 BEFORE any model error**, hence sub-pixel refinement. ⚠️ **Same structure as CV ch. 09 §6's RoI align: quantisation invisible at box precision is fatal at keypoint precision.**
- **ch. 10 — TRIPLET LOSS IS CV ch. 03 §7's HINGE ON DISTANCES, SO IT SATURATES IDENTICALLY — AND THE COMBINATORICS MAKE THAT FATAL.** Zero loss once the negative is a margin further than the positive ⇒ satisfied triplets give **exactly no gradient**. With 10,000 identities × 20 images there are **$7.60\times10^{11}$ triplets** and almost all are already satisfied ⇒ ***sampling uniformly means sampling ZERO GRADIENT: hard-negative mining is not an optimisation, it is the only way training progresses.*** **THIRD setting for one failure** — CV ch. 03 §7 ("a model that separates the data stops learning") and DL ch. 06 §8's detection imbalance — **and the fixes rhyme: mining here, focal loss there, and ArcFace's angular-margin softmax is exactly the "replace the hinge with cross-entropy" move.**
- **ch. 10 — TOP-DOWN POSE IS $O(\#\text{people})$ AND BOTTOM-UP IS $O(1)$, CROSSOVER AT ~2 PEOPLE.** At 50 people bottom-up is **17× faster**; a crowd of 100 is 100× for one and 1× for the other. **The trade is accuracy for scaling**: top-down gets a full-resolution crop per person (far better on small/distant people), bottom-up runs once and must **GROUP** keypoints — *and grouping fails exactly in the crowded scenes it was chosen for.* ⇒ **precisely CV ch. 07's two-stage/one-stage trade in a different task: per-instance processing buys accuracy and costs linear scaling.** *(Also: left/right confusion is the characteristic pose failure and is a GLOBAL-information problem — like CV ch. 02 §7's shadow-vs-object edge, no local detector can fix it, which is why the skeleton prior is in the model rather than in post-processing.)*
- **ch. 09 — THE CHECKERBOARD ARTIFACT IS PURE GEOMETRY: TRANSPOSED-CONVOLUTION OVERLAP IS UNIFORM IFF $s$ DIVIDES $k$.** Counted interior overlaps for eight $(k,s)$ pairs: **$k{=}3,s{=}2 \to \{1,2\}$ (a 2× difference between adjacent output pixels), $k{=}5,s{=}3 \to \{1,2\}$; $k{=}2,s{=}2$, $k{=}4,s{=}2$, $k{=}6,s{=}3$ all uniform.** ⇒ ***the periodic intensity pattern is baked into the arithmetic BEFORE any weights are learned*** — it is not a training pathology. **Fixes: choose $k$ divisible by $s$ (4/2 is standard), or upsample-then-convolve, which has no overlap structure at all.** *(Odena et al. 2016 identified the artifact; counting the overlaps to show it is weight-independent is the addition.)*
- **ch. 09 — PIXEL ACCURACY 80.0% AGAINST mIoU 37.7% ON IDENTICAL PREDICTIONS — A FACTOR OF 2.12, WITH THE CLASSES THAT MATTER AT EXACTLY ZERO.** Constructed road scene (road 35%, building 25%, sky 20%, vegetation 12%, car 5%, person 2%, sign 1%): a model that gets the top three right and calls everything else "road" scores **80% pixel accuracy** while **car, person and sign score IoU 0.0000** (and road's own IoU falls to 0.6364 because absorbing other classes counts as false positives). ⇒ ***mIoU averages over CLASSES, so a 1%-of-pixels class weighs as much as a 35% one — that is exactly why it is standard and pixel accuracy is not.*** *Same structure as DL ch. 06 §8's detection imbalance, and CV ch. 03's "accuracy on an imbalanced set is meaningless" made quantitative.*
- **ch. 09 — DICE $= 2\,\mathrm{IoU}/(1+\mathrm{IoU})$ EXACTLY, AND THE INFLATION PEAKS AT $\mathrm{IoU} = \sqrt2 - 1$.** Verified to $10^{-12}$ on random mask pairs. **Dice > IoU always (for $0<\mathrm{IoU}<1$), and maximising $\frac{2I}{1+I}-I$ gives $\frac{2}{(1+I)^2}=1 \Rightarrow I = \sqrt2-1 \approx 0.4142$, gap $= 0.1716$** — *largest exactly in the mid-range where most real results sit.* ⇒ ***the two rank models IDENTICALLY and are NOT comparable as numbers: a "0.59 Dice" and a "0.41 IoU" are the same model.*** **Always state which.**
- **ch. 09 — A STRIDE-32 BACKBONE PREDICTS 49 VALUES PER CHANNEL FOR 50,176 PIXELS — 1,024 PIXELS PER PREDICTED VALUE — AND UPSAMPLING CANNOT INVENT INFORMATION.** ⇒ **that single ratio is the whole argument for SKIP CONNECTIONS: the high-resolution features were never destroyed, only bypassed, so re-inject them rather than trying to reconstruct them.** U-Net makes this the architecture — **39,225,600 values bypass the bottleneck** across four skip levels. ***Same insight as ResNet's identity path (DL ch. 05 §14): do not force a network to re-derive something it already has*** — ResNet adds, U-Net concatenates, and DenseNet showed concatenation's quadratic channel cost. *This is also CV ch. 05 §3's activation-volume halving read backwards: classification discards detail on purpose and segmentation has to undo it.*
- **ch. 09 — RoI ALIGN MATTERS ONLY FOR MASKS, AND THERE IT IS DECISIVE, FOR THE SAME REASON AS THE CHECKERBOARD.** RoI pooling ROUNDS region coordinates to the feature grid; **at stride 16 a half-cell error is 8 input pixels.** ⇒ ***quantisation that is invisible in a bounding box is fatal in a pixel mask*** — bilinear interpolation instead of rounding is the single change that made instance masks work (He et al. 2017). *DL ch. 06 §9 reproduced both of RoI pooling's printed outputs; this is why its successor exists.*
- **ch. 08 — THE SAME DETECTIONS GIVE AP = 0.6833, 0.7045 OR 0.6865 DEPENDING ONLY ON THE INTERPOLATION CONVENTION.** Worked a full 8-detection / 5-object example: all-point (COCO, VOC2010+) **0.683333**, 11-point (VOC2007) **0.704545**, 101-point (COCO) **0.686469** — **a 3.1% spread from the PROTOCOL alone, before any modelling**, and larger than many reported improvements. ⇒ ***"mAP" is not a number until you name the protocol AND the IoU threshold.*** ⚠️ **And AP is capped by RECALL**: only 4 of 5 ground-truth objects were ever detected, so recall caps at 0.80 and **AP is bounded above by 0.80 however the scores are ordered** — *misses cost at every threshold and cannot be recovered by re-ranking.*
- **ch. 08 — AP@0.5 IS 1.55× THE COCO HEADLINE AP@[.5:.95], AND THE GAP IS A LOCALISATION DIAGNOSIS.** Averaging over IoU 0.50…0.95 takes 0.620 → **0.399**; quoting one against the other is a **55% error**, and both are called "mAP". ⚠️ **The classifier is IDENTICAL across those thresholds, so the 95% collapse from IoU 0.5 to 0.95 measures LOCALISATION QUALITY ALONE** ⇒ *a large gap means the detector finds the right objects and boxes them loosely — fix the loss weight, the box parametrisation or the feature stride, NOT the classifier or the data.*
- **ch. 08 — FOCAL LOSS SWINGS DL ch. 06's OWN MEASURED IMBALANCE BY A FACTOR OF 2,501, AND THAT IS WHY ONE-STAGE DETECTORS CAUGHT UP.** $-(1-p_t)^\gamma\log p_t$ down-weights an easy negative at $p_t{=}0.99$ by **10,000×**. Applied to DL ch. 06 §8's measured case (1 hard positive vs **5,443** easy negatives): **plain CE gives the negatives 78.9× more total loss than the positive; focal $\gamma{=}2$ inverts it so the POSITIVE dominates by 31.7×** — a **2,501× swing from one exponent**. ⇒ ***the one-stage/two-stage accuracy gap was never architectural: a two-stage detector's proposals pre-filter background, and focal loss removes the same imbalance from the OBJECTIVE instead of from the data.*** ⚠️ **$\gamma$ has a narrow window — at $\gamma{=}5$ the negatives contribute $4\times10^6$× less and the model stops learning what background looks like at all.**
- **ch. 08 — DETR ANSWERS TWO OF THE THREE STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCES WITH ONE MECHANISM, COMPLETING THE VAULT'S DELETION PATTERN.** $N{=}100$ learned queries give the fixed-size output (consequence i), and **one-to-one Hungarian matching makes a duplicate an explicit false positive**, so the model **learns not to duplicate** instead of being filtered afterwards (consequence iii). **Anchors gone, NMS gone.** Matching is tractable only because a polynomial algorithm exists: **$O(N^3)=10^6$ against $1.3\times10^{39}$ brute-force matchings at $m{=}20$.** ⇒ **this completes DL ch. 06 §9's pattern (SIFT → learned kernels; fc head → global average pooling; selective search → RPN; anchors+NMS → set prediction) by removing NMS — the one component that had survived BOTH the classical and the CNN eras intact.** ⚠️ **Costs are real: $N$ is a HARD cap (fine for COCO's ~93-object maximum, impossible for a 200-person crowd, by construction not by training), and convergence is slow because the matching is unstable before any query specialises.**
- **ch. 08 — FPN AND MULTISCALE ANCHORS ARE DIFFERENT THINGS AND ONE NAME HIDES IT.** FPN's top-down path (1×1 laterals + upsample + 3×3 smoothing) costs **3,344,384 parameters = 12.76 MB ≈ 13.1% of a ResNet-50** and gives **every pyramid level the same SEMANTIC DEPTH**; DL ch. 06 §6's multiscale gives each level a different **OBJECT SIZE**. ⇒ **they are complementary, and a detector may have one without the other.** *This is also why CV ch. 06 §4 found Swin's HIERARCHY — not its windowing — is what made Transformers usable as detection backbones: a plain ViT has one resolution and no pyramid to build on.*
- **ch. 07 — DETECTION'S OUTPUT IS A VARIABLE-LENGTH SET OVER A CONTINUOUS SPACE, AND THE THREE THINGS EVERY DETECTOR DOES ARE THREE ANSWERS TO THAT ONE FACT.** Classification outputs a fixed $K$-vector; detection outputs $\{(c_i,\mathbf b_i)\}$ of unknown size — **$80^3 = 512{,}000$ class-assignments for three objects, $3.3\times10^9$ for five, and the boxes are real-valued so the space is not even countable.** ⇒ **(i) no fixed-size output layer → anchors / proposals / queries; (ii) the loss cannot be computed until predictions are MATCHED to ground truth → assignment (IoU-greedy, or Hungarian in DETR); (iii) duplicates are possible → NMS, or a loss that forbids them.** ***Anchors, assignment and NMS are not three unrelated tricks — they are three answers to one structural problem***, and seeing that makes the two-stage and one-stage families look like variations rather than rival lineages.
- **ch. 07 — THE EXHAUSTIVE SLIDING WINDOW IS $1.16\times10^{15}$ OPERATIONS FOR ONE $224^2$ IMAGE, AND THAT NUMBER EXPLAINS THE WHOLE FIELD.** Scales 32–224 at stride 1: **94,535 square windows, 283,605 with 3 aspect ratios**; at a ResNet-50 pass each (~$4.1\times10^9$ ops) that is a **quadrillion operations per image**. Stride 16 gives 1,365 windows — **208× fewer** — at the cost of ±8 px localisation error, *which is exactly what offset regression exists to repair*. ⇒ ***every detector is a scheme for evaluating far fewer than 283,605 windows***: R-CNN ~2,000 proposals, SSD 5,444 anchors, DETR 100 queries. *And CV ch. 02's HOG+SVM ran this exact pipeline — feasible only because its classifier was a 3,780-dim dot product rather than a CNN.*
- **ch. 07 — THE LOSS WEIGHT $\lambda$ IS AN INVENTED EXCHANGE RATE BETWEEN INCOMMENSURABLE UNITS, AND IT DECIDES WHAT THE MODEL OPTIMISES.** $L = L_{cls} + \lambda L_{loc}$ mixes **nats** with **normalised box units** — there is no natural conversion. On a typical positive anchor ($L_{cls}{\approx}0.69$, $L_{loc}{\approx}0.15$): **$\lambda{=}0.1$ → 97.9% classification / 2.1% localisation; $\lambda{=}10$ → 31.5% / 68.5%.** ⇒ ***$\lambda$ silently decides whether the model prefers to be right about WHAT or about WHERE*** — boxes in the right place with wrong labels, or correct labels with sloppy boxes, **both of which look like "needs more training" and neither of which is.** ⚠️ **And this is the SECOND imbalance in the same scalar**: DL ch. 06 §8 measured the first at **123:1 to 5,443:1 foreground:background with the mask applied only to the OFFSET half.** **Two different imbalances, one loss, neither visible in the loss curve.**
- **ch. 06 — ATTENTION OVERTAKES THE FEED-FORWARD NETWORK AT EXACTLY $n > 2d$, AND AT ViT's OWN DESIGN POINT IT IS A MINORITY OF THE WORK.** Per layer with $d_{ffn}{=}4d$: attention $= 4nd^2 + 2n^2d$, FFN $= 8nd^2$ ⇒ crossover $n > 2d$ = **1,536 tokens for ViT-Base ($d{=}768$) = a $624\times624$ image**. Attention's share: **36.1% at $224^2$**, 50.5% at $640^2$, 64.7% at $1024^2$, **85.4% at $2048^2$**. ⇒ ***"attention is quadratic" is asymptotically true and MISLEADING at 224² — the FFN dominates there, so optimising attention buys at most 36%.*** **The quadratic only becomes the problem for DENSE PREDICTION at high resolution, which is exactly where Swin is used.** *This is DL ch. 08 §8's $n<d$ crossover specialised to vision and evaluated.*
- **ch. 06 — ViT-BASE COMES TO 86,528,488 PARAMETERS, INDEPENDENTLY MATCHING THE UNIVERSALLY QUOTED ~86M, AND THE FFN/ATTENTION SPLIT IS EXACTLY 66.7%/33.3%.** Per layer: attention $4d^2 = 2{,}359{,}296$, FFN $= 4{,}722{,}432$, LayerNorm 3,072 → 7,084,800 × 12 layers + patch embedding 590,592 + positions 151,296 + head 769,000. **330.1 MB fp32, 3.4× ResNet-50.** ⇒ ***DL ch. 08 §10's inversion holds in vision — THIRD setting for the pattern (after CV ch. 05's conv-vs-head and DL ch. 08's language Transformer): the component the architecture is NAMED AFTER holds a third of its weights.***
- **ch. 06 — PATCH SIZE IS THE CRITICAL HYPERPARAMETER BECAUSE THE PENALTY IS QUADRATIC.** $224^2$ gives 196 tokens at $P{=}16$, 784 at $P{=}8$, 3,136 at $P{=}4$ ⇒ **halving $P$ quadruples $n$ and multiplies attention work by 16 (256× at $P{=}4$).** *That is CV ch. 05 §2's resolution dial with a SQUARE-LAW penalty instead of a linear one — which is why ViT ships /32, /16, /14 and why /8 is rare.* **And the patch embedding IS a $P\times P$ convolution with stride $P$: ViT is not "a Transformer with no convolution", it is a Transformer with EXACTLY ONE convolutional layer, whose receptive fields never overlap.**
- **ch. 06 — SWIN'S WINDOW SAVING IS EXACTLY $n/M$, SO IT GROWS PRECISELY WHERE IT IS NEEDED — AND IT GIVES BACK THE ONE PROPERTY ATTENTION WAS FOR.** Full $O(n^2d)$ vs window $O(nMd)$: **64× at $224^2$, 334× at $512^2$, 1,338× at $1024^2$.** *Unusually, the optimisation improves as the problem worsens.* **The cost is PATH LENGTH**: a token sees one window per layer, so DL ch. 08 §8's max path length of 1 — *the property that made attention worth having* — is lost, and shifted windows restore coverage only OVER DEPTH: **~10 layers for a $56\times56$ token grid, ~43 for $256\times256$.** ⇒ **that real cost is why ViT and Swin coexist rather than one replacing the other.** *Swin's second idea is pure CNN import: merge $2\times2$, halve resolution, double channels — which is what makes it a multi-scale detection backbone, not the windowing.*
- **ch. 06 — THE INDUCTIVE-BIAS TRADE, MEASURED, AND IT IS A GENERAL LAW.** Layer-1 receptive field: **CNN $3\times3$ = 0.02% of a 224² image; ViT self-attention = 100%** (DL ch. 08 §8's path length 1, in vision). **The price: translation equivariance and locality must be LEARNED.** Published pattern — ViT **underperforms** a ResNet at ImageNet-1k (1.3M), **matches** at ImageNet-21k (14M), **overtakes** at JFT-300M ⇒ **crossover between $10^7$ and $10^8$ images.** ⇒ ***a weaker prior needs more data and buys more flexibility once the data exists — and WHICH SIDE OF THE CROSSOVER YOU ARE ON IS A PROPERTY OF YOUR DATASET, NOT OF THE ARCHITECTURE.*** **So "ViT beats CNNs" is conditional on a data threshold most projects never reach** — hence almost nobody trains one from scratch, and hence the lecturer's project topics are all scoped to fine-tuning. *And ConvNeXt (2022) ran it in reverse — a modernised pure CNN matching Swin — showing much of ViT's advantage was the TRAINING RECIPE, not the attention.*
- **ch. 05 — THE DEPTHWISE-SEPARABLE SAVING IS $1/c_o + 1/k^2$, WHICH ASYMPTOTES TO $k^2$.** Standard $k^2c_ic_o$ vs separable $k^2c_i + c_ic_o$. **At $k{=}3$: 5.76× at $c_o{=}16$, 7.89× at 64, 8.69× at 256, 8.92× at 1024** — *the $1/c_o$ term is negligible past 64, so the saving is a property of the KERNEL SIZE, not the layer width.* **Larger kernels save MORE: 22.8× at $k{=}5$, 41.1× at $k{=}7$** — which is exactly why depthwise designs use $5\times5$ and $7\times7$ freely where dense ones cannot. ⇒ **and it is DL ch. 05 §15's GROUPED CONVOLUTION AT THE EXTREME $g = c_i$** (one channel per group, 256× fewer $3\times3$ params at $c{=}256$), **with the following $1\times1$ restoring cross-channel mixing — ResNeXt's sandwich, for the identical reason: no information crosses groups.**
- **ch. 05 — EFFICIENTNET'S PUBLISHED CONSTANTS GIVE 1.9203, NOT 2, AND THE ERROR COMPOUNDS.** FLOPs scale as $d\cdot w^2\cdot r^2$ (width scales BOTH channel counts, resolution BOTH spatial dims). With the paper's $\alpha{=}1.2,\beta{=}1.1,\gamma{=}1.15$: $\alpha\beta^2\gamma^2 = \mathbf{1.9203}$ ⇒ **4.0% low per step, compounding to 21.7% by $\phi{=}6$ (50.1× the base, not 64×).** ⚠️ **NOT filed as an erratum** — Tan & Le write $\approx 2$ with an approximation sign and the constants come from a constrained grid search, so *the paper is correct and the approximation is simply looser than it reads.* ⇒ **recompute $\alpha\beta^2\gamma^2$ before budgeting compute.** *And compound beats single-axis because the dials buy DIFFERENT things: depth buys nonlinearity and receptive field (saturating once the RF covers the image — DL ch. 05 §7's VGG-11 reaches 150 of 224 px), width buys features per position, resolution buys detail to see.*
- **ch. 05 — THE ACTIVATION VOLUME HALVES AT EVERY STAGE; IT DOES NOT STAY CONSTANT, AND THE COMMON CLAIM IS WRONG BY 2× PER STAGE.** Channels double ($\times2$) while resolution quarters ($\times\frac14$) ⇒ $C\cdot H\cdot W \times \frac12$: **200,704 → 100,352 → 50,176 → 25,088, a factor of 8 across three stages.** *Keeping the volume constant would need channels to QUADRUPLE.* ⇒ **the EARLY layers dominate activation memory** (DL ch. 04 §5), so **input resolution is the most expensive hyperparameter in a CNN — $r^2$ in FLOPs AND $r^2$ in activations** — and gradient checkpointing pays off first in the early stages.
- **ch. 05 — THREE CHECKS THAT CATCH MOST ERRORS IN AN ARCHITECTURE SPEC** (the lecturer's own "how to read an architecture table" framing): (i) **spatial arithmetic** $\lfloor(\text{in}+2p-k)/s\rfloor+1$; (ii) **the shortcut must match the main path in $C$, $H$ AND $W$** — which is *why* a $1\times1$ stride-2 projection appears exactly when a stage downsamples (DL ch. 05 §14: $\mathbf x + F(\mathbf x)$ needs matching shapes, the same requirement that forces $d_{model}$ constant in a Transformer); (iii) **track $C\cdot H\cdot W$** and expect it to halve. *Worked ResNet stage $(64,56,56)\to(128,28,28)$: 73,856 + 256 + 147,584 + 256 + 8,320 = **230,272** params, with the shortcut only 3.6% of it.*
- **ch. 04 — AN MLP CANNOT SEE SPATIAL STRUCTURE AT ALL, AND THAT IS A THEOREM.** $\big(\mathbf W_{[:,\pi]}\big)\big(\mathbf x_\pi\big)=\mathbf W\mathbf x$ **exactly, for every permutation $\pi$ and every $\mathbf W$** — verified to $1.07\times10^{-14}$ over 200 random trials. ⇒ ***shuffle every pixel of every image with ONE fixed permutation and an MLP learns the identical function at the identical accuracy*** — the permutation is absorbed into the first layer's columns. **The images become unrecognisable to a human and the MLP does not notice: it treats an image as an UNORDERED BAG of numbers.** The same shuffle makes a convolution's output **unrelated** ($\|\Delta\|_F = 17.38$; 24 responses forming two clean vertical edges become 44 scattered ones). ⇒ **locality is not an efficiency trick, it is the mechanism by which spatial structure enters the model at all.** *The usual "too many parameters" objection is the WEAK one — memory is purchasable, permutation invariance is not repairable.*
- **ch. 04 — THE BINDING CONSTRAINT IS DATA, NOT MEMORY, AND THAT INVERTS THE USUAL PRESENTATION.** To be translation-invariant an MLP must learn the same feature independently at **every** position: **900 copies on $32\times32$, 49,284 on $224\times224$** ($3\times3$ pattern). Learning $N$ independent copies needs roughly $N$ times the examples — an edge detector at the top-left corner learns **nothing** from an edge at the centre. ⇒ **~$10^4$–$10^5$× more labelled data, which does not exist**, whereas the 2.30 GB of weights is merely expensive. ***Weight sharing is a statement about SAMPLE EFFICIENCY first and memory second.*** *(Same reuse as DL ch. 05 §17's "a conv weight does $h\cdot w$ times more WORK per parameter", counted in examples instead of FLOPs.)*
- **ch. 04 — THE PARAMETER EXPLOSION AT SIZES THAT ACTUALLY MATTER, AND THE ADVANTAGE GROWS WITH RESOLUTION.** One fc layer: CIFAR-10→4096 is 12.6M params (48 MB); **ImageNet $224^2\times3$→4096 is 616,566,784 params = 2.30 GB, or 9.19 GB to train with Adam** (DL ch. 04 §5); a 4K frame→1000 is **92.70 GB**. The same first layer as a convolution (64 filters, $3\times3$) is **1,792 parameters — 344,066× fewer** (DL ch. 05 §8 got 23,671× for LeNet; this is an order of magnitude further out). **And the two-assumption cascade scales: 65,536× at $32\times32$, $1.6\times10^8$ at $224\times224$, $6.2\times10^{10}$ at one megapixel.** ⇒ ***CIFAR-scale benchmarks understate the convolutional advantage by six orders of magnitude — they systematically flatter architectures that scale badly with resolution.***
- **ch. 04 — THREE INDEPENDENT ROUTES NOW REACH THE SAME OPERATOR, AND THE VAULT HAS WALKED ALL THREE.** (i) **impose translation invariance + locality on an MLP** → convolution, $10^{10}$ parameters saved (DL ch. 05 §1); (ii) **assume linearity + shift-invariance** → convolution, forced by theorem (CV ch. 02 §3); (iii) **observe that an MLP is permutation-invariant** → convolution, because nothing else uses arrangement (CV ch. 04 §2). ⇒ ***three independent arguments converging on one operator is why the convolution is not a design choice.*** **General lesson: when a second and third route reach the vault's existing conclusion, record the ROUTE — the convergence is the finding.**
- **ch. 03 — THE CURSE OF DIMENSIONALITY KILLS k-NN ON PIXELS, AND THE NUMBER IS BRUTAL.** With 1,000 uniform random points, $(d_{\max}-d_{\min})/d_{\min}$ is **2,574.66 at $d{=}1$, 3.44 at $d{=}10$, 0.475 at $d{=}100$ and 0.0656 at CIFAR-10's $d{=}3072$** — *the farthest point is only **6.6%** further than the nearest.* **Mechanism, separated: the mean distance grows like $\sqrt d$ (1.44 → 22.60, ×15.7) while the standard deviation stays essentially CONSTANT (0.2475 → 0.2238)**, so sd/mean falls 0.1714 → 0.0099 like $1/\sqrt d$. ⇒ ***the nearest neighbour is nearest by a margin comparable to noise — it is chosen by accident.*** **And it reconciles with Cover & Hart's (1967) optimality theorem: that needs the sample DENSE in the space, which needs $N$ exponential in $d$. The theorem is true and vacuous here.**
- **ch. 03 — k-NN's COST PROFILE IS EXACTLY BACKWARDS, and the ratio is $N/K$.** Training $O(1)$, prediction $O(ND)$: **153,600,000 operations per CIFAR-10 image vs 30,720 for a linear classifier — 5,000×, and that is $N/K$ exactly, INDEPENDENT of image size** (so smaller images do not help). Whole test set = $1.54\times10^{12}$ operations. **And k-NN must ship the training set AS its model: 146.5 MB uint8 / 585.9 MB float32, against the linear model's 30,730 parameters = 0.12 MB = 0.02% of it.** ⇒ ***learning is compression; a model that does not compress is a lookup table.***
- **ch. 03 — 'INVERTING AN IMAGE PRESERVES THE CATEGORY' IS A PROOF, NOT A HINT.** For **any** $\mathbf w$, $\mathbf w^\top(-\mathbf x) = -\mathbf w^\top\mathbf x$ **identically** ⇒ **no linear model can score an image and its photographic negative highly at once.** ch. 01 §1's remark plus this identity **proves the model class is wrong** — no data or training fixes it. *Translation breaks it just as completely: one $2\times2$ blob shifted 0–3 px scored $+1.09, +0.97, -0.19, -1.08$ against a fixed $\mathbf w$ — four views of one object, four unrelated scores, including a sign flip.* ⇒ **a linear classifier on raw pixels is invariant to NONE of ch. 01's eight variations. That is the whole argument for ch. 04.**
- **ch. 03 — THE HINGE LOSS SWITCHES OFF AND THE CROSS-ENTROPY NEVER DOES.** Hinge is **exactly 0** once every margin exceeds $\Delta$ — those examples contribute **no gradient at all** — while cross-entropy decays like $e^{-\text{margin}}$: $5.51\times10^{-1}$ at margin 1, $1.34\times10^{-2}$ at 5, $9.08\times10^{-5}$ at 10, $4.12\times10^{-9}$ at 20, **never zero**. ⇒ **with the hinge a model that separates the data STOPS LEARNING; with cross-entropy it keeps growing margins forever** — which is why softmax is the default *and* why regularisation is then compulsory. *A "read what the code prints" catch: at margin 50 the printed cross-entropy was `-0.000000`, which is **signed-zero underflow**, not zero (true value $\approx e^{-50}\approx2\times10^{-22}$).*
- **ch. 03 — SCALING $\mathbf W$ MAKES A MODEL MORE CONFIDENT WITHOUT MAKING IT MORE ACCURATE, AND THAT IS SZELISKI'S CALIBRATION WARNING MADE NUMERICAL.** Scores $[10,9,9]$ scaled by 10: top probability **0.5761 → 0.9999**, entropy **0.9753 → 0.0010 nats**, and the `argmax` — hence the accuracy — **never changes**. Meanwhile the **hinge stays exactly 0 at every scale** ($\times2,\times10,\times100$) ⇒ **the data loss alone does not determine $\|\mathbf W\|$: an entire ray $\{\alpha\mathbf W:\alpha\ge1\}$ is equally optimal, which is what $\lambda\|\mathbf W\|^2$ is for.** Szeliski: the losses *"do not, in fact, encourage the network outputs to be properly confidence calibrated"* (Guo et al. 2017), and his fix — dividing logits by a temperature — **is literally undoing this scaling.** ⇒ ***a softmax output is a SCORE until someone calibrates it; "the model was 99% sure" reports the norm of $\mathbf W$.***
- **ch. 03 — L2 IS ROTATION-INVARIANT AND L1 IS NOT, AND PIXELS HAVE NO PRIVILEGED AXES.** Over 2,000 random orthogonal $\mathbf Q$: **L2 spread $2.2\times10^{-15}$ (machine precision); L1 spread 3.7378 = 54.0% of its own mean.** ⇒ **L1 asserts the coordinate axes are meaningful — true for tabular features, false for pixels**, where rotating the image or changing colour space is exactly a change of frame. *And Szeliski's footnote generalises it: standardizing and whitening change distances too ⇒ **"the distance between two images" is a property of your preprocessing, not of the images.***
- **ch. 02 — HOG's 3,780 DIMENSIONS DERIVE EXACTLY, AND THE OVERLAP IS THE WHOLE POINT.** $105$ blocks $\times 36$ values $= \mathbf{3{,}780}$ ✓ (cells $8\times16=128$; blocks $(8-2+1)\times(16-2+1)=7\times15$). **The raw cell histograms are only $128\times9 = 1{,}152$ numbers — the descriptor is 3.28× larger purely because blocks overlap.** Counted the multiplicity: **corner cells are normalised once, edge cells twice, INTERIOR CELLS FOUR TIMES**, each against a different local contrast. ⇒ ***the redundancy IS the illumination invariance*** — and without overlap the descriptor would be exactly 1,152, i.e. the raw data with no invariance bought at all. **The slide states 3,780 and nothing else.**
- **ch. 02 — THE SLIDE'S OWN OUTPUT-SIZE FORMULA IS DEEP LEARNING ch. 05's, AND SO IS ITS ODD-KERNEL CONSEQUENCE.** $H_{out}=\lfloor(H+2p-f)/s\rfloor+1$ with ‘same’ padding $p=(f-1)/2$ — **an integer only for odd $f$** ($f{=}4$ needs $p{=}1.5$). The lecture's own aside, ‘Same formula you will use for every CNN layer’, is the point: **there is no separate CNN arithmetic.** ⇒ ***the same conclusion reached independently from the classical-filtering side and the CNN side.*** *Likewise §3's theorem — ‘linear + shift-invariant ⇒ the operation IS a convolution, there is no other choice’ — is the converse of DL ch. 05 §1, which IMPOSED translation invariance on an MLP and measured the payoff at $10^{10}$ parameters.* **Two routes, one operator.**
- **ch. 02 — THE MEDIAN'S BREAKDOWN POINT IS 50% AND THE MEAN'S IS 0%, AND BREAKDOWN IS A CLIFF.** On a 9-pixel patch: 1 corrupted pixel moves the mean **+13.1** and the median **+0.0**; **4 of 9 (44%) move the mean +53.1 and the median STILL +0.0**; at 5 of 9 the median jumps **+122.0** and fails completely. **And the two filters fail in DIFFERENT WAYS, not by different amounts**: on a single impulse a 3-tap Gaussian turns **1 contaminated pixel into 3** (peak 255 → 127.5) while the median **deletes it entirely (1 → 0)**. ⇒ ***a Gaussian SPREADS each impulse; the median DELETES it — choose by the noise model, not by reputation.*** *Cost, checked at 1080p: naive $O(HWk^2\log k)$ vs histogram-based $O(HWk)$ is 4.8× at $k{=}3$ and 58.6× at $k{=}15$ — the naive implementation is what makes people think the median is slow.*
- **ch. 02 — SOBEL IS AN OUTER PRODUCT AND SEPARABILITY IS A SAVING THAT GROWS.** $[1,2,1]^\top[-1,0,1]$ reproduces $S_x$ exactly; $S_y=S_x^\top$. **$k^2 \to 2k$ multiplications: 1.5× at $k{=}3$, 3.5× at $k{=}7$, 7.5× at $k{=}15$.** Gaussian, box, Sobel, Prewitt and derivative-of-Gaussian are all separable; **the bilateral filter is not, because its weights depend on the image — which is exactly the price of edge preservation.** *The three operators differ ONLY in the smoothing row: Prewitt $[1,1,1]$ puts 33.3% on centre, Sobel $[1,2,1]$ 50.0%, Scharr $[3,10,3]$ **62.5%**.*
- **ch. 02 — THE LoG FORMULA IS SYMBOLICALLY EXACT AND THE DoG APPROXIMATION IS ONLY GOOD NEAR $k=1$.** $\nabla^2G_\sigma - \frac{x^2+y^2-2\sigma^2}{\sigma^4}G_\sigma = \mathbf 0$ identically (sympy). **But the DoG/LoG ratio of maxima is 0.868 at $k{=}1.1$, 0.508 at 1.6 and 0.375 at $k{=}2$ — off by a factor of 2.7.** ⇒ **that is why SIFT uses $k=2^{1/s}$ per octave, deliberately close to 1**, and why the approximation is worth making at all: **DoG is two SEPARABLE blurs vs one non-separable LoG — 2.2× cheaper at $9\times9$, 6.2× at $25\times25$.** *The slide states both formulas and evaluates neither.*
- **ch. 02 — THE CLASSICAL DETECTION PIPELINE IS DEEP LEARNING ch. 06's WITH THE LEARNED PARTS REMOVED.** HOG+SVM: **sliding window → anchors; image pyramid → feature pyramid; SVM score → classification head; NMS → NMS, unchanged.** ⇒ ***modern detection kept the scaffolding and replaced the features*** — the same ‘delete a hand-designed stage’ pattern recorded in DL ch. 06 §9, and **NMS is the one component that survived both eras intact.** *Recognising this makes ch. 07 much shorter.*
- **ch. 02 — TWO SMALL RULES WORTH KEEPING, both verified on a constant image.** (i) **A derivative kernel MUST sum to 0 and a smoothing kernel MUST sum to 1** — Laplacian on a constant image returns 0 to machine precision, normalised Gaussian returns the input exactly. *An instant sanity check on any kernel you write.* (ii) **Gradient magnitude is NOT illumination-invariant**: under $I \to aI$ it scales by $a$ — *‘hence the normalization steps in HOG and SIFT’*, one sentence that explains a design choice in both.
- **ch. 02 — FOUR PHYSICALLY DIFFERENT CAUSES PRODUCE IDENTICAL EDGE PIXELS**: depth discontinuity, surface-orientation change, reflectance change, illumination change. **‘A shadow edge and an object edge look identical locally — a permanent limitation of purely local methods.’** ⇒ *no edge detector can be fixed to solve this, because the information is not in the neighbourhood* — which is one concrete, honest answer to ‘why deep learning’. **Related: Canny's HYSTERESIS is the only non-local step in the whole classical pipeline, and it is exactly what beats a single threshold.**

- **ch. 01 — THE LECTURER'S OPENING CLAIM IS QUANTITATIVE AND IT CHECKS OUT TO THE ORDER OF MAGNITUDE.** Slide 13: vision "predates language by five orders of magnitude." **Eyes ≈540 Myr, writing ≈5 kyr ⇒ 540×10⁶/5×10³ = 108,000 = 10⁵·⁰³ — five orders exactly.** Against *speech* (~100 kyr) it is 5,400 = 10³·⁷³. ⇒ **the claim is true for writing and overstated by 1.3 orders for speech**, and the slide says "language", which usually means speech. *Worth stating precisely rather than repeating.*
- **ch. 01 — THE GRAYSCALE WEIGHTS SUM TO EXACTLY 1, AND THAT IS THE POINT.** $0.299+0.587+0.114=1.000$ exactly, so grayscale is an **affine-preserving convex combination**: white stays white. **Green carries 58.7% and blue 11.4% — a 5.15× ratio** reflecting cone sensitivity. ⇒ *a "brightness" channel is a weighted opinion about human eyes, not a physical measurement.*
- **ch. 01 — PERSPECTIVE PROJECTION IS WHY VISION IS AN INVERSE PROBLEM, AND IT IS ONE LINE.** $x=fX/Z$, $y=fY/Z$ maps $\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^2$: **every point on a ray through the pinhole lands on the same pixel, so $Z$ is destroyed.** The whole subject is the attempt to recover what that division threw away.
- **ch. 01 — THE PINHOLE TRADE-OFF IS A GENUINE DILEMMA AND THE LENS IS THE RESOLUTION.** Small hole → sharp, poor SNR; large hole → bright, blurry. **A lens breaks it by focusing rather than blocking** — at the price of the thin-lens constraint $1/z_o+1/z_i=1/f$, which can hold for **only one $z_o$ at a time** ⇒ *defocus is not a defect, it is the arithmetic of the equation.*
